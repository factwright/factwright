# The browser extension

Factwright's extension for Chrome and Firefox, called Factwright in both stores. It was Save to Factwright at first, as its Firefox ID (`save@factwright.com`) and its listings' addresses still say. On a job's page, click its button, and save it in one click, or check its details first: the job lands in your Factwright job hunt as New, with its link and its posting as you saw it. A job you saved already shows as it's on file, with its status, and a job you saved from another page, as a Hacker News post or a company's whole board, gets this page's link and posting instead of a copy. On an unsaved job's application, Save and fill saves it and fills the form, and Save only saves it alone, to fill later. It saves straight to Factwright on the same computer, with no account, or through sync, from any computer: Factwright's sync, or a sync server of your own. It reads pages that need a sign-in too, since it reads what's on your screen, LinkedIn included, unless you turn that off.

It also fills in a job's application form, for you to check and submit yourself: click Fill this application in the popup, in the right-click menu, or with Alt+Shift+F. It reads the form, gets an answer for each field, from Profile, the job's own resume and what you sent before, fills the form in its page, in place of what's there wherever it has an answer, and uploads the job's resume and cover letter under their own names, but never over a file you attached. A small note on the page says how it went, "Factwright filled 14 fields · 2 need you · Details", and what's left for you, and needed, is outlined in orange. Details, on the note or in the popup, opens a panel beside the page that lists every field: the job at the top, then what needs you, then what it filled, folded away, each with where its answer came from. Mark as applied keeps what you sent with the job. It never submits anything, and on LinkedIn and Indeed, whose terms forbid filling their forms, the panel only gives answers to copy. An application that runs over several pages in one tab, as Workday's does, is filled a page at a time: you press its own Save and Continue, and Fill again, or the panel offers to fill the next page. A part of it the site filled with another job, from your resume, stays as it is, and where a page has fewer parts for jobs or schools than Factwright has on file, as Workday's My Experience, the panel offers to add one for each, pressing the form's own Add only when you choose it.

On the same computer, it asks Factwright for the answers, and your Claude can draft the form's new questions there at once. Set up through sync, on a computer without Factwright, it answers them itself, from your job hunt, as Factwright would: it reads what sync holds with the account's keys, in memory, for the fill, and keeps none of it. Your street, postal code and equal-opportunity answers never sync, so they're left for you, and the form's new questions are left with the job, for your Claude in Factwright to draft. Mark as applied offers to keep a new answer you typed in Profile, through sync too, but for those three.

On the job application sites you allow, and only there, it also shows its answers in a form's fields as you fill it in: turn them on in setup, or from the panel when you fill a form. One optional permission covers the sites where the applicant systems most companies use show their forms, and any other careers site can be added on its own. There, a small Fill button on a page with an application form fills it as Fill this application does, and a field you click into, where Factwright has an answer, shows a menu beside it with each answer on file, in the extension's own frame, which the page can't read: or, for one of the form's own questions with none, Draft with Claude, which drafts all of the form's open questions at once. Nothing goes in a field until you pick an answer. A menu goes beside its field where it covers none of the page's own controls, and just under the field, over the page, where it would, as a password manager's does. Leaving a field with a new answer Profile can keep, such as a new phone number, offers to save it. As you submit the form, it keeps what you sent, and once the page says the application went in, or moves on with no errors showing, it marks the job Applied, with an Undo note. Elsewhere, the popup has Mark as applied. A company's careers page that shows the form in a frame from one of those sites gets the button and the menus in the frame, and Fill fills the form there. It needs Chrome 130 or later, the first to give its frame an address of each session's own.

Setup asks where saved jobs go. **Factwright on this computer** needs nothing but Factwright: the browser hands each job to a small helper Factwright installs and registers with each browser it finds, through the browser's native messaging, and Factwright adds it when it's open ([apps/desktop/helper](../desktop/helper)). **Through sync**, it's a device on your sync account, as a linked browser is: it signs in through the browser's own window for extensions, where you allow it on your sync server's page, and Factwright on your computer approves linking it, after you check both show the same emoji. A server that doesn't know this copy signs it in with a code you approve in your browser instead. Everything it saves through sync is locked with your job hunt's key before it leaves the browser. [docs/security.md](../../docs/security.md#the-browser-extension) has the design.

## What it may do
- **activeTab and scripting:** read the tab you're on, once, when you click its button, its menu item or its shortcut, and the frames in it from the same site, as iCIMS's career sites show each page in. Nothing else, and nothing as you browse. The popup saves nothing until you click: to say whether you saved the job already, it asks Factwright on this computer about the page's address, company and job title, and nothing else, or reads what sync holds.
- **storage:** keep its settings.
- **contextMenus:** its Save job to Factwright and Fill this application items in the page's right-click menu.
- **sidePanel** (Chrome): the panel beside the page where filling a form is checked. Firefox's sidebar needs no permission. Neither shows a warning.
- **declarativeContent** (Chrome): rules Chrome checks itself, which light its button up on pages that look like an application form, so the extension reads nothing to light it. No warning either.
- **identity:** the browser's own window to sign in to sync through, which hands the answer to this extension alone, so there's no code to compare. Neither browser shows a warning for it, at install or on an update. It never reads your Google account or email.
- **nativeMessaging** (optional): asked for only when you choose Factwright on this computer in setup, fill a form while saving there, or choose Use Factwright on this computer in the panel as you fill one through sync, to hand each job you save, and each ask as it fills a form, to Factwright's helper, `com.factwright.save`, and to nothing else. Each browser lets only this extension reach that helper. It's given back once you set the extension up to save through sync instead, which fills forms from your job hunt through sync without it. An update that brings it asks nothing of anyone, as it's optional.
- **app.factwright.com:** Factwright's sync, the one site it reaches unless you choose a server of your own. Both browsers grant it at install, and let you take it away later: setup then asks for it back.
- **The job application sites** (optional, each named, and any https site you add): asked for only when you turn them on in setup or the panel, or add a careers site there, and then for those sites alone, so the browser asks you once, for them. There, and only there, the extension registers its script, in each of their pages, and in a frame from one of them in another site's page, there alone, never on LinkedIn or Indeed. Turned off, or taken away in the browser's settings, its script is unregistered, and stops on pages that are open. Signing in or out of sync never gives them back. An update that brings them asks nothing of anyone, as they're optional.
- **Its own frame** (web-accessible resources, `frame.html` alone): the Fill button and the menus the script shows on those sites, which the page can't read. Its address is one no site can guess: Chrome's changes each time Chrome starts, and Firefox's is the one Firefox gave the extension at install. So a site where it never shows can't load it to tell the extension's there, unless a site where it shows passes the address on.
- **A server of your own** (optional, `https://*/*`): asked for only when you choose Your own server in setup, and then for the address you type alone, so the browser asks you once, for that one site. It keeps that permission only while it's signed in there, or signing in: it's given back when you sign out, when signing in there fails or you cancel it, and when it signs in somewhere else. A wider permission you allow yourself in the browser's settings, such as all https sites, is yours, and it never takes that back. Its sign-in and everything it saves go only to the server it signed in to, and with your own, it reaches nothing of Factwright's.

Its only script in pages is the one it registers on the job sites you allowed, and only there, so elsewhere it reads nothing as you browse, and no site can tell it's installed or message it. Filling a form reads only the page you clicked Fill on, or a frame of it from a job site you allowed, and while the panel shows that form, what's in its fields every few seconds, so it has what you sent once you submit it. On a job site you allowed, its script reads what the form's fields hold as you submit it, or press one of its pages' Save and Continue, for the background, which keeps only the answers to the form's own questions, in memory, until the page says how it went, and sends them only as it marks the job Applied.

Filling a form through sync opens your job hunt with the account's keys: every record's name, the job tracker's rows, Profile, your work history, and only the job's own `job.md`, resume, cover letter, drafts and PDFs among its files, with the `job.md` of any job folder no row names, as from Factwright 0.15 or earlier. The first fill reads all of it, and each fill after, only what changed. What it opens stays in the memory of the panel or the background that fills forms, and goes 5 minutes after its last use, or as it's next asked once the extension's signed out: nothing it reads is ever stored. What it filled a form with, its notes on the page, and what you sent until the page says how it went stay in the background's own memory alone, never in the browser's storage, until the tab closes or the browser stops the background. What it leaves with a job, a form's questions and what you sent, and a new answer you save to Profile, is locked in the browser before it's sent, as everything it saves is.

On the job sites you allowed, its script sees each field you can see, with its question and options, as Fill reads them, to put a Fill button on a page with an application form. The first time you click or tab into one of the form's fields, it sends those fields, never what's in them, to Factwright on this computer, which answers them as it does Fill, or answers them from your job hunt through sync, as Fill does there, and the answers stay in the extension: the page's script gets only the one you pick, to put in the field, and only then. With Factwright on this computer, it reads what's in a field only once you've typed in it, or picked one of its options, and left it, and only for a field whose answer Profile keeps, to offer to keep a new answer, never a password or an equal-opportunity answer. Through sync, it reads none, as it offers to keep none. And as you submit the form, by a click of your own on its Submit, or its Save and Continue, or Enter in one of its boxes, it reads what all its fields hold, but a password, for Applied, as above. A menu opens only for a field you clicked or tabbed into yourself, takes only your own clicks, once it's shown for a moment, puts nothing in the field if the page faded or covered it as you picked, and is the same size however many answers it has. Equal-opportunity questions get no menu: Fill picks their answers, as you set them in Profile, as the panel does. Those sites can tell the extension is there, as its frame is on the page, and whether Factwright has an answer for a field, as a menu shows. Through sync, it holds your job hunt's keys, which open all of it, as each linked device does: [docs/security.md](../../docs/security.md#the-browser-extension) says what that means.

A build for another sync server (`VITE_FACTWRIGHT_SYNC_SERVER`) must name an `https://` address, or this computer's, and gets no zips, so only a build for Factwright's own sync can go to the stores. Setup offers that server as Factwright's sync, and in such a build a server of your own can also be this computer's over http, for testing. The stores' build takes only https.

## Trying it
```bash
pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build
```

It makes `dist/chrome` and `dist/firefox`, and a zip of each.
- **Chrome:** open `chrome://extensions`, turn on Developer mode, then Load unpacked and pick `dist/chrome`.
- **Firefox:** open `about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox`, then Load Temporary Add-on and pick `dist/firefox/manifest.json`. It stays until Firefox restarts.

Setup opens on its own. A copy loaded this way signs in to Factwright's sync with a code, since its server knows only the stores' copies by their IDs, and Chrome gives a copy loaded from a folder an ID of its own. Firefox gives it the stores' ID, so it signs in through the window. A server's `EXTENSION_IDS` can add a copy's ID ([apps/server](../server/README.md#the-extensions-window)).

To try saving to Factwright on this computer, build the desktop app's helper (`pnpm --filter @factwright/desktop helper`), then run the desktop app from the code with the unpacked extension's ID, from `chrome://extensions`, so it registers the helper for it as well as the stores' ID. On a Mac, then set the browser up in the app's Browser extension window, from its sidebar, as macOS asks first:

```bash
FACTWRIGHT_EXTENSION_ID=<the unpacked extension's ID> pnpm dev
```

To try it with a sync server of your own, choose Your own server in setup and type its address. A server of your own signs it in through the window alone, never with a code, since it could pass along a code Factwright's sync gave it, so a Chrome copy loaded this way needs its ID in that server's `EXTENSION_IDS`, which setup names. One running locally is reached over http, which only a build for another server takes, so build it with that server's address, or any on this computer:

```bash
VITE_FACTWRIGHT_SYNC_SERVER=http://localhost:8787 pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build
```

The tests run with the rest (`pnpm test`); `test/save.test.ts` saves jobs to the real sync server, run locally. The end-to-end run needs Playwright's Chromium, once (`pnpm --filter @factwright/site exec playwright install chromium`), and a C compiler, to build the desktop app's helper for testing (on a Mac, Xcode's command line tools):

```bash
pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e
```

With `-- --shots <folder>`, it also saves each setup page it checks, in light and dark, to look over a change.

Firefox has a run of its own for saving to Factwright on this computer, as Playwright's Firefox can't load extensions: it drives a real Firefox over WebDriver BiDi, with the stores' build, the desktop app's helper built for testing, and the app's inbox code. Firefox finds the helper through a manifest in its home folder, so the run gives Firefox a temporary home of its own: through `HOME` on Linux, as CI runs it, and on a Mac through `CFFIXED_USER_HOME`, as a Mac's Firefox finds its Library folder from that rather than from `HOME`. It never writes to your own, where your Factwright registers its helper. Before it saves anything, it checks that Firefox finds no helper while the run has registered none: if Firefox ever read your folder instead, it would find your Factwright's helper, and the run stops there, having sent it only a hello, which changes nothing. On Windows, Firefox finds the helper through your own registry, so the run says so and stops. It needs Mozilla's Firefox 158 or later, not Ubuntu's snap, and a C compiler:

```bash
pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e:firefox
```

Filling a form has a run of its own in Chromium, as `e2e` does, with the desktop app's helper and its inbox and fill code, and the job application sites, which its build has from install (`FACTWRIGHT_JOB_SITES_AT_INSTALL`), as Chromium can't answer the prompt for them in a test, and which count this computer's site among them in a build for testing; `-- --shots <folder>` saves the panel, the filled form, the Fill button and a field's menu there, on the form's page and in a careers page's frame:

```bash
pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e:fill
```

Filling a form through sync has a run of its own in Chromium, with a local copy of the sync server, as `e2e` and the server's tests run one, and the desktop app's own sync putting a made-up job hunt there. It needs no C compiler, as there's no Factwright on the computer it fills on. `-- --shots <folder>` saves the panel, the filled form and a field's menu there:

```bash
pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e:sync-fill
```

In Firefox, `e2e:firefox-fill` sends the built form function into forms served on this computer through the extension's own scripting, as its sidebar does, with the same Firefox 158 or later that `e2e:firefox` needs: it reads, fills, never a password, picks a place from Lever's suggestions and presses Ashby's No, uploads a file through Firefox's content-script sandbox, and fills two pages of a Workday application, through its dropdowns, its lists you search and its date boxes, and presses its Add Another. Then it turns the job application sites on in setup, which Firefox grants on the click with its prompt for optional permissions turned off, checks the script is registered and puts its Fill button on the form, presses it, does the same in a careers page's frame, reading the form there by the frame's number, and turns the sites off again. With no Factwright helper registered, the button says it couldn't find Factwright, and the run checks Firefox finds no helper first. Before turning them off, it links the extension through sync to a made-up job hunt on a local sync server, with a link code in setup, and the Fill button, with no Factwright there, fills the form from the job hunt and uploads its resume.

`FIREFOX=<path>` runs another Firefox than the one on PATH, or on a Mac than the one in Applications: CI runs Mozilla's 158 that way, since its runners' own is older. When Firefox can't reach the helper, it says "No such native application com.factwright.save" for one that isn't registered, or isn't for this extension, and only "An unexpected error occurred" for one that isn't where its manifest says, as when Factwright was moved to the trash. Setup reads both as Factwright not found, as it does Chrome's words.

## Publishing it
Both stores take the zip that `build` makes. [store/README.md](store/README.md) has what each asks for, in words to paste, and the steps.
- **Chrome Web Store:** a developer account (a one-time $5), then upload `dist/factwright-chrome-<version>.zip`.
- **Firefox Add-ons:** a free account, then upload `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. Mozilla asks for the source too, since the code is bundled: a zip of this repository at the release's commit, with these steps to build it.

Raise `version` in `package.json` for each new upload: both stores take each version once.
