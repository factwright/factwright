# Publishing the browser extension

What the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons ask for, ready to paste, and the steps. Build first: `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build` makes `dist/factwright-chrome-<version>.zip` and `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The screenshots are in `screenshots/`, drawn by `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e -- --screenshots`.

Before the first upload, the sync server and factwright.com must be deployed with this version: the server takes the extension's sign-in, and the privacy policy describes it. The same goes for the first upload that lets you choose a server of your own: factwright.com's privacy policy says it may reach one. And for the first upload that saves to Factwright on the same computer, the Factwright release that carries its helper comes out first, and factwright.com's privacy policy describes it: without that release, setup finds no Factwright to save to.

Version 0.2.0 signs in through the browser's own window wherever the server knows the stores' copies. With Factwright's sync it signs in with a code where the server doesn't, so it can go out before or after the sync server that knows them: until that server is deployed, it signs in with a code, as 0.1.x does. A server of your own signs it in through the window alone, never with a code, so a server of your own needs a version with the window. sync-server has published one with each release since Factwright 0.9.0, so every one deployed from there has it.

Version 0.3.0 is the first the stores list as Factwright, and the first that fills in applications. Upload it once all three are done:
1. **Factwright 0.15.0 is out.** It carries the app's side of what 0.3.0 does, such as each answer on file for a field's menu to offer.
2. **factwright.com is deployed with the extension page and privacy policy that describe it:** filling applications, the job application sites, forms in another site's frame, and Get the form's questions. Set the privacy policy's effective date to the day it's deployed.
3. **Firefox's data collection is chosen,** and the manifest says it ([Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030)).

It needs Chrome 130 or later, so Chrome keeps 0.2.0 for anyone on an older one.

## The listing
Name no other company in anything you paste into either store: no applicant tracking systems, no job sites and no other brands, not even in the answers and notes only reviewers read. The Chrome Web Store rejected the first submission for keyword spam (violation Yellow Argon) over "LinkedIn, Indeed, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and other job sites". So the listing calls Factwright's Claude "your own AI assistant", and the sites whose terms forbid filling their forms "the job sites whose terms forbid it". `test/store.test.ts` checks every quoted block here for those names, and that neither browser names the other.

**Name:** Factwright

**Summary** (both stores, at most 132 characters), the same as the manifest's description:
> Save the job you’re looking at to your Factwright job hunt, and fill in its application for you to check.

**Description:**
> This extension brings your Factwright job hunt into your browser: it saves the job you're looking at, and, with Factwright on the same computer, fills in its application for you to check and send.
>
> Saving a job: on its page, click the Factwright button, check the title, company and posting, and save it. Or press Alt+Shift+S, or right-click the page, to save it without opening anything. It reads the job details a page publishes, the layout of the job boards and applicant tracking systems people use most, or the text you selected, so it also saves postings you can only see when signed in. The job shows in Factwright on your computer as New, ready for a fit check, a tailored resume or a cover letter, with the posting kept even after the listing comes down.
>
> Filling its application: click Fill this application, in the popup or the right-click menu, or press Alt+Shift+F, and a panel opens beside the page. Factwright on the same computer answers each field you can see from what you've told it: your profile, the job's own resume and cover letter, and what you sent before. The panel lists every field with its answer and where it came from, and what's left for you is outlined on the page. It picks a place from the suggestions a location box offers, presses a form's Yes and No buttons, and fills a form that a company's careers page shows in a frame. It never submits: you check the form and send it yourself.
>
> Drafts of the rest: Factwright can have your own AI assistant draft the questions nothing on file answers, such as why you want the job, and the panel fills each draft in for you to check. What only you can answer, it leaves for you.
>
> Right in the form, on the sites you allow: turn on the job application sites, in setup or the panel, and on those sites alone a small Fill button sits on an application form, and a field you click into shows a menu with your answers on file, which the site can't read. Nothing goes in a field until you pick an answer. One optional permission covers the sites where the applicant tracking systems most companies use show their forms, and you can add any careers site.
>
> Private by design:
> • It reads a page only when you click its button, its menu item or its shortcut, and nothing as you browse, except on the job application sites you allow. The popup sends nothing until you save.
> • Filling a form, it asks Factwright on the same computer for the answers, with the form's questions and never what's in them, and no server in between. It never fills a password or payment details, and never agrees to anything for you.
> • On the job sites whose terms forbid filling their forms, it fills nothing, and gives you answers to copy.
> • Saved to Factwright on the same computer, a job goes straight to the app, never to a server. Through sync, what it saves is locked on your computer with your job hunt's own key before it's sent, so the sync server stores it in a form it can't read.
> • It reaches no site but your sync server, Factwright's or one you run yourself, and has no analytics or tracking.
>
> The extension costs nothing to add. It needs Factwright, the free desktop app: factwright.com. It saves straight to Factwright on the same computer, with no account, or through sync from any computer, with Factwright's sync or a server of your own, and fills applications with Factwright on the same computer. Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync. Factwright is open source: github.com/factwright/factwright

Both stores ask a listing to say when any of what an extension does needs something paid, so both carry the price: "Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync." If the price changes, change it here, on factwright.com and in both listings together.

Firefox Add-ons reads the description as Markdown, and an edit there goes live as soon as it's saved. Paste it with a blank line after "Private by design:", and each point starting with "- " instead of "• ". Chrome's description is plain text, as above, and a change to it waits for the store's review.

While the browser runs, it also keeps which frames its script is in (storage.session), so the Fill button still works after the background restarts.
   - **contextMenus:**
     > For the Save job to Factwright and Fill this application with Factwright items in the page's right-click menu.
   - **sidePanel:**

It never reads the account the browser is signed in to, or its email (no identity.email or getAuthToken).

Nothing is asked at install: when the user chooses "Factwright on this computer" in setup, or clicks Fill this application, the extension asks for it with permissions.request. It talks only to Factwright's own helper, com.factwright.save, which the Factwright app installs and registers for this extension's ID alone. When the user asks for drafts, it sends the form's own questions that nothing on file answers, for the app to have the user's own AI assistant draft. When the user marks a job applied, it sends their answers to the form's own questions, to keep with the job, and when they choose Save for a new answer, such as a new phone number, that one answer, for the app to keep. It gives the permission back if the user sets it up to save through sync instead.

A sync server of their own, which is open source: when the user chooses "Your own server" in setup and types its address, the extension asks for that one origin (permissions.request), checks it answers as a Factwright sync server, and saves through it instead of app.factwright.com. It gives the permission back when the user signs out, or when signing in there fails or is canceled. And a careers site of the user's choosing: from the side panel, the user can add the site of a company's careers page, one at a time, which asks for that one origin, for the extension's script to run there as on the job application sites below. It never asks for any other site.

     - **Authentication information:** the extension's own sign-in to the user's sync account.
     - **Web history:** the address of the page they saved a job from, or filled a form on.

## Firefox Add-ons
1. Sign in at https://addons.mozilla.org/developers/ with a Mozilla account, turn on its two-step authentication, and accept the developer agreement.
2. **Submit a New Add-on,** on this site (listed), then upload `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The manifest's ID is `save@factwright.com`, and it needs Firefox 140 or later, the first to show what an extension sends at install. Firefox has taken the optional host permission for servers of your own since 128, and native messaging as an optional permission since 87.
3. **Source code:** yes, the code is bundled. Upload a zip of the repository at the commit the zip was built from (`git archive --format=zip -o factwright-source.zip HEAD`), and paste these steps:
   > Node.js 24 and pnpm 12.10.1 (`corepack enable`). From the repository's root: `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, then `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build`. The add-on is `apps/extension/dist/firefox`, and its zip is `apps/extension/dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The code isn't minified.
4. **Listing:** the name, summary, description, categories, screenshots, homepage, support and privacy policy above. Under the license, AGPL-3.0-only. Once Factwright sells sync, a version from before servers of your own, which can save only through Factwright's sync, also needs `requires_payment` marked ("requires payment, non-free services or software"). From 0.2.0, the version with servers of your own, it doesn't, and that's the version Firefox has listed since October 19, 2026.
5. **Data collection:** the manifest's `data_collection_permissions`, which Firefox shows at install and on the listing. It declares browsing activity (the address of a job the user saves or a form they fill), website content (its posting, and a form's questions) and authentication information (its sync sign-in) as required. Before uploading 0.3.0, choose whether to add personally identifying information: [Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030), below.
6. **Notes to reviewer:**
   > This extension adds the job in the current tab to the user's Factwright job hunt, and, with the Factwright desktop app on the same computer, fills in the job's application form for the user to check and submit themselves. It has no remote code, no analytics, and no content scripts in its manifest.
   >
   > Saving: it reads a tab only when the user clicks its button, its context menu item or its keyboard shortcut (activeTab with scripting.executeScript of one function, readJobPage in src/page/read-job.ts, which reads the page without changing it). The popup sends nothing until the user saves. It saves through Factwright's own sync server, end-to-end encrypted (app.factwright.com, its one host permission at install), or through a Factwright sync server the user runs themselves. For that, it declares the optional host permission https://*/*: only when the user chooses "Your own server" in setup and types its https address does it request that one origin with permissions.request (src/setup.ts, src/account.ts). It then checks the server answers /api/sign-in-options as a Factwright sync server before signing in, sends nothing to any other site, and removes the permission when the user signs out, or the sign-in fails or is canceled. It signs in with identity.launchWebAuthFlow (src/account.ts): Firefox's window opens the sync server's own page, where the user allows it, and the server's answer comes back to the extension's identity.getRedirectURL() address with a one-time code the extension trades for its session, with PKCE (packages/sync/src/extension-sign-in.ts). A server that doesn't know the extension's return address gets a device code instead.
   >
   > Factwright on this computer: it can also save to the Factwright desktop app on the same computer, with no account. For that, it declares nativeMessaging as an optional permission, and requests it with permissions.request only when the user chooses "Factwright on this computer" in setup, or clicks Fill this application (src/setup.ts, src/computer.ts). It talks only to com.factwright.save, a helper the Factwright desktop app installs and registers in ~/Library/Application Support/Mozilla/NativeMessagingHosts, or in the registry under HKCU\Software\Mozilla\NativeMessagingHosts, allowing save@factwright.com alone. It sends each saved job as an object of six strings (url, site, company, role, pay, posting) and nothing else, and each ask as it fills a form as an object of two strings (type "ask" and request, src/computer.ts). The helper only writes a job into the app's data folder, and passes an ask only to the app while it's open (its source is apps/desktop/helper).
   >
   > Filling: the popup, the context menu item and the keyboard shortcut open its sidebar (sidebar_action), which reads that tab's form with scripting.executeScript of one function, formPage in src/page/form.ts, reading each visible field's question, options and limits, and never what's in it, and asks the app what to put in each field, from the user's profile, resumes and past answers there. It fills only fields the user can see, never a password or payment details, never agrees to anything, and never submits the form. A box that suggests places as the user types gets their city typed in, as they would, and the matching suggestion picked with a click, and a form's Yes and No buttons are clicked as they would. While the sidebar shows the form, it reads what's in the form's own questions every few seconds, so that when the user marks the job applied it can give the app their answers to them. When the user asks the sidebar for drafts, it sends the app the form's own questions that nothing on file answers, and the app has the user's own AI assistant draft them, which the sidebar fills in once they're done. It never fills the forms of the job sites whose terms forbid it (COPY_ONLY_SITES in src/applicant-systems.ts), and only gives answers to copy there.
   >
   > The job application sites: only when the user turns them on, in setup or the sidebar, does it request them with permissions.request (the optional host permissions the manifest lists, src/applicant-systems.ts), or, from the sidebar, one https careers site the user adds. It then registers its one script, content.js (src/job-site-script.ts), on those sites alone with scripting.registerContentScripts, in their pages and in frames from them on other sites' pages (allFrames), never in the page around such a frame, and unregisters it when they're turned off or the permission is removed (src/application-sites.ts). That script reads each visible field's question as formPage does, never a password, and shows a Fill button and, in a field the user clicks into, a menu with the extension's answers, in an iframe of the extension's one web-accessible page, frame.html, which the site can't read. It puts an answer in a field only when the user picks it in that menu, and reads what's in a field only once the user has typed in it and left it, for a field the desktop app's profile keeps, to offer to keep the new answer there, which it does only when the user clicks Save. A click in a page's frame can't open the sidebar, so the Fill button fills the form in place, with the same code, unless the sidebar is already open. On a page that shows such a form in a frame, the sidebar reads and fills the form in that frame, by its frame ID. The script never runs on the job sites whose terms forbid filling their forms.
   >
   > To try it with no account: install Factwright from factwright.com and open it, choose Browser extension in its sidebar, and Set up beside Firefox if it offers it, then choose Factwright on this computer in the extension's setup. To try saving through sync, a Factwright account with sync turned on in the desktop app is needed, which approves linking the extension: ask in the review's thread for a test account. The source is public at https://github.com/factwright/factwright, under apps/extension.

### Data collection in 0.3.0
A choice for you before 0.3.0 goes up: the manifest's `required` list stays as 0.2.0 has it until you make it. Firefox shows what's declared at install, on the listing and in `about:addons`: today, "The developer says this extension collects: browsing activity, website content, authentication information". Mozilla's policies count as collection "any data collected, used, transferred, shared, or handled outside the add-on or the local browser", so Factwright on the same computer counts, as 0.2.0's declaration already takes it.

What 0.3.0 hands on that 0.2.0 didn't:
- **To Factwright on the same computer:** a form's questions as it fills it, which website content covers. The user's answers to a form's own questions, when they mark a job applied. And a new answer they choose to keep in Profile, from the panel or a field's menu: a phone number, an email address, a link, their street, city or postal code, or one of their equal-opportunity answers (gender, ethnicity, race, veteran status or disability).
- **Into the page they're filling, at their click:** their contact details, work history and education from Profile and their resumes, and the equal-opportunity answers they set in Profile. The page's own code can read what's filled before they submit it, and a location box's code sends what's typed there to its site, to look up places.

Mozilla's examples for `personallyIdentifyingInfo` are "contact information such as name and address, email, and phone number, as well as other identifying data such as ID numbers, voice or video recordings, age, demographic information, or biometric data", so both carry it. The choices:
- **A. Leave it as it is.** The install prompt and the listing stay the same, and the update asks no one anything. But they leave out the contact details and equal-opportunity answers the extension hands the app and puts in forms, which Mozilla's definition counts. A reviewer could turn 0.3.0 down for it, or Mozilla could ask for it later, when more people have the extension to ask.
- **B. Add `personallyIdentifyingInfo` to `required`.** One line in `src/manifest.ts`, with its comment. The install prompt and the listing then say "The developer says this extension collects: browsing activity, website content, authentication information, personally identifying information". Everyone with 0.2.0 gets a prompt when 0.3.0 reaches them, "Factwright requires new settings to update", with "The developer says the extension will collect: personally identifying information", and Firefox keeps 0.2.0 until they accept. (`updatePromptHandler` in Firefox's `AddonManager.sys.mjs` holds an update that adds a required data type, as it does one that adds a permission.) It matches the Chrome answers above and the privacy policy.
- **C. B, and `healthInfo` too.** Mozilla's examples for it are "medical history, symptoms, diagnoses, treatments, procedures, or heart rate data". The one answer near it is a disability answer on an equal-opportunity form, which Fill picks only as the user set it in Profile, and the panel keeps only if they choose Save. B reads it as a demographic self-identification, which personally identifying information covers. C reads it as health information too, and the prompts then say "health information" to everyone, for an answer most people never set.
- **D. Make `personallyIdentifyingInfo` optional.** Nothing changes at install or in the update. The extension would ask for it the first time the user fills a form, with `permissions.request({ data_collection: ['personallyIdentifyingInfo'] })` from the popup's or the sidebar's click, since a click in a page's frame can't ask, and fill no contact details, nor keep a new answer, without it. That's new code for Firefox alone, and a second prompt. `about:addons` then lists it as "Share personally identifying information with extension developer", which isn't where it goes.

**Recommended: B, before 0.3.0 goes up.** It says what the extension does, in Mozilla's terms. The update prompt reaches only the people who have 0.2.0 now, which Firefox has listed since October 19, while adding it later would ask everyone who has it then. If you read a disability answer as health information, take C, and tick Health information in Chrome's answers too.

## Once it's live

## Each update
Raise `version` in `apps/extension/package.json`, build, and upload the new zip to each store (with a new source zip for Firefox). Note what changed in the CHANGELOG. Paste the listing and the answers above again where they changed since the last upload: for 0.3.0, all of them.

0.3.0, the version that fills forms, asks nothing of people with 0.2.0 in Chrome. `sidePanel` and `declarativeContent` are Chrome's alone, and show no warning. The job application sites are optional host permissions, which the browser asks for only when someone turns them on, and `frame.html` is a web-accessible resource, which asks nothing at an update. Chrome 127 to 129 keep 0.2.0, as 0.3.0 needs Chrome 130. Firefox asks nothing either, unless the update adds a required data type ([Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030)): then it asks everyone first, and keeps the old version until they accept.

A new optional permission, such as `https://*/*` for servers of your own or `nativeMessaging` for Factwright on this computer, asks nothing of people when the update reaches them: the browser asks only when they choose to use it. A new required one with a warning would ask everyone, and Chrome turns the extension off until they accept. `identity`, new in 0.2.0, is required, since Firefox can't take it as optional, and shows no warning in either browser, so the update asks nothing. A managed Chrome whose administrator blocks the `identity` permission (`ExtensionSettings`' `blocked_permissions`) won't load the extension.
