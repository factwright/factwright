# Publishing the browser extension

What the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons ask for, ready to paste, and the steps. Build first: `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build` makes `dist/factwright-chrome-<version>.zip` and `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The screenshots are in `screenshots/`, drawn by `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e -- --screenshots`.

Before the first upload, the sync server and factwright.com must be deployed with this version: the server takes the extension's sign-in, and the privacy policy describes it. The same goes for the first upload that lets you choose a server of your own: factwright.com's privacy policy says it may reach one. And for the first upload that saves to Factwright on the same computer, the Factwright release that carries its helper comes out first, and factwright.com's privacy policy describes it: without that release, setup finds no Factwright to save to.

Version 0.2.0 signs in through the browser's own window wherever the server knows the stores' copies. With Factwright's sync it signs in with a code where the server doesn't, so it can go out before or after the sync server that knows them: until that server is deployed, it signs in with a code, as 0.1.x does. A server of your own signs it in through the window alone, never with a code, so a server of your own needs a version with the window. sync-server has published one with each release since Factwright 0.9.0, so every one deployed from there has it.

Version 0.3.0 is the first the stores list as Factwright, and the first that fills in applications. Upload it once both are done:
1. **Factwright's release after 0.15.0 is out.** Between them they carry the app's side of what 0.3.0 does: 0.15.0 has each answer on file for a field's menu to offer, and the next has how many jobs and schools are on file, for the panel to offer a part for each on an application that runs across several pages. Filling through sync needs nothing new from the app: the next release also names each job's folder in its synced row, so a fill through sync downloads less, but 0.3.0 fills just as well without it.
2. **factwright.com is deployed with the extension page and privacy policy that describe it:** filling applications, the job application sites, forms in another site's frame, and Get the form's questions. Set the privacy policy's effective date to the day it's deployed.

It needs Chrome 130 or later, so Chrome keeps 0.2.0 for anyone on an older one. In Firefox, it declares personally identifying information too, so Firefox asks everyone with 0.2.0 before it updates them ([Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030)).

## The listing
Name no other company in anything you paste into either store: no applicant tracking systems, no job sites and no other brands, not even in the answers and notes only reviewers read. The Chrome Web Store rejected the first submission for keyword spam (violation Yellow Argon) over "LinkedIn, Indeed, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and other job sites". So the listing calls Factwright's Claude "your own AI assistant", and the sites whose terms forbid filling their forms "the job sites whose terms forbid it". `test/store.test.ts` checks every quoted block here for those names, and that neither browser names the other.

**Name:** Factwright

**Summary** (both stores, at most 132 characters), the same as the manifest's description:
> Save the job you’re looking at to your Factwright job hunt, and fill in its application for you to check.

**Description:**
> This extension brings your Factwright job hunt into your browser: it saves the job you're looking at, and fills in its application for you to check and send, from Factwright on the same computer or from your job hunt through sync.
>
> Saving a job: on its page, click the Factwright button, check the title, company and posting, and save it. Or press Alt+Shift+S, or right-click the page, to save it without opening anything. It reads the job details a page publishes, the layout of the job boards and applicant tracking systems people use most, or the text you selected, so it also saves postings you can only see when signed in. The job shows in Factwright on your computer as New, ready for a fit check, a tailored resume or a cover letter, with the posting kept even after the listing comes down.
>
> Filling its application: click Fill this application, in the popup or the right-click menu, or press Alt+Shift+F, and a panel opens beside the page. It answers each field you can see from what you've told Factwright: your profile and work history, the job's own resume and cover letter, and what you sent before. The panel lists every field with its answer and where it came from, and what's left for you is outlined on the page. It picks a place from the suggestions a location box offers, picks from a form's own lists, presses its Yes and No buttons, and fills a form that a company's careers page shows in a frame. An application that runs across several pages fills a page at a time: you go on with the form's own button, the panel offers to fill the next page, and what the site already filled in stays as it is. It never submits: you check the form and send it yourself.
>
> On a computer without Factwright: set up through sync, the extension fills from your job hunt itself, which it opens with your own keys, in memory, for the fill, and stores none of it. Your street, postal code and equal-opportunity answers never sync, so it leaves those for you. Once you've applied, it can keep a new answer you typed, such as a new phone number, in your profile through sync, if you choose Save, but never one of those three.
>
> Drafts of the rest: Factwright can have your own AI assistant draft the questions nothing on file answers, such as why you want the job, and the panel fills each draft in for you to check once it's done, or, through sync, at your next fill after it syncs. What only you can answer, it leaves for you.
>
> Right in the form, on the sites you allow: turn on the job application sites, in setup or the panel, and on those sites alone a small Fill button sits on an application form, and a field you click into shows a menu with your answers on file, which the site can't read. Nothing goes in a field until you pick an answer. One optional permission covers the sites where the applicant tracking systems most companies use show their forms, and you can add any careers site.
>
> Private by design:
> • It reads a page only when you click its button, its menu item or its shortcut, and nothing as you browse, except on the job application sites you allow. The popup saves nothing until you click. To show whether you saved a job already, it asks Factwright on the same computer about the page's address, company and title alone, or reads your synced jobs in your browser.
> • Filling a form on the same computer, it asks Factwright for the answers, with the form's questions and never what's in them, and no server in between. Through sync, it reads your job hunt from your sync server, opens it with your own keys in your browser alone, and forgets it a few minutes after the fill.
> • It never fills a password or payment details, never agrees to anything for you, and never presses a form's button to go on or submit.
> • On the job sites whose terms forbid filling their forms, it fills nothing, and gives you answers to copy.
> • Saved to Factwright on the same computer, a job goes straight to the app, never to a server. Through sync, what it saves is locked on your computer with your job hunt's own key before it's sent, so the sync server stores it in a form it can't read.
> • It reaches no site but your sync server, Factwright's or one you run yourself, and has no analytics or tracking.
>
> The extension costs nothing to add. It needs Factwright, the free desktop app: factwright.com. It saves jobs and fills applications straight with Factwright on the same computer, with no account, or through sync from any computer, with Factwright's sync or a server of your own. Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync. Factwright is open source: github.com/factwright/factwright

Both stores ask a listing to say when any of what an extension does needs something paid, so both carry the price: "Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync." If the price changes, change it here, on factwright.com and in both listings together.

Firefox Add-ons reads the description as Markdown, and an edit there goes live as soon as it's saved. Paste it with a blank line after "Private by design:", and each point starting with "- " instead of "• ". Chrome's description is plain text, as above, and a change to it waits for the store's review.

     > To keep the user's settings: where saved jobs go, the job application sites they allowed, and whether it reads postings on a site whose terms forbid copying. ## Firefox Add-ons
1. Sign in at https://addons.mozilla.org/developers/ with a Mozilla account, turn on its two-step authentication, and accept the developer agreement.
2. **Submit a New Add-on,** on this site (listed), then upload `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The manifest's ID is `save@factwright.com`, and it needs Firefox 140 or later, the first to show what an extension sends at install. Firefox has taken the optional host permission for servers of your own since 128, and native messaging as an optional permission since 87.
3. **Source code:** yes, the code is bundled. Upload a zip of the repository at the commit the zip was built from (`git archive --format=zip -o factwright-source.zip HEAD`), and paste these steps:
   > Node.js 24 and pnpm 12.10.1 (`corepack enable`). From the repository's root: `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, then `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build`. The add-on is `apps/extension/dist/firefox`, and its zip is `apps/extension/dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The code isn't minified.
4. **Listing:** the name, summary, description, categories, screenshots, homepage, support and privacy policy above. Under the license, AGPL-3.0-only. Once Factwright sells sync, a version from before servers of your own, which can save only through Factwright's sync, also needs `requires_payment` marked ("requires payment, non-free services or software"). From 0.2.0, the version with servers of your own, it doesn't, and that's the version Firefox has listed since October 19, 2026.
5. **Data collection:** the manifest's `data_collection_permissions`, which Firefox shows at install and on the listing. All four are required: browsing activity (the address of a job the user saves or a form they fill), website content (its posting, and a form's questions), authentication information (its sync sign-in), and, from 0.3.0, personally identifying information (the user's details it puts in a form, a new answer they choose to keep, which goes in their profile through sync too, and what they sent, kept with the job through sync). [Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030), below, says why.
6. **Notes to reviewer:**
   > This extension adds the job in the current tab to the user's Factwright job hunt, and fills in the job's application form for the user to check and submit themselves, from the Factwright desktop app on the same computer or from the user's job hunt through sync. It has no remote code, no analytics, and no content scripts in its manifest.
   >
   > Saving: it reads a tab only when the user clicks its button, its context menu item or its keyboard shortcut (activeTab with scripting.executeScript of one function, readJobPage in src/page/read-job.ts, which reads the page without changing it, and in a frame of the same site where an applicant system shows its page in one). The popup saves nothing until the user chooses to. To show whether the job is saved already, it sends the Factwright app on this computer the page's address, company and job title, through native messaging, or reads the user's synced jobs, decrypted in the browser. It saves through Factwright's own sync server, end-to-end encrypted (app.factwright.com, its one host permission at install), or through a Factwright sync server the user runs themselves. For that, it declares the optional host permission https://*/*: only when the user chooses "Your own server" in setup and types its https address does it request that one origin with permissions.request (src/setup.ts, src/account.ts). It then checks the server answers /api/sign-in-options as a Factwright sync server before signing in, sends nothing to any other site, and removes the permission when the user signs out, or the sign-in fails or is canceled. It signs in with identity.launchWebAuthFlow (src/account.ts): Firefox's window opens the sync server's own page, where the user allows it, and the server's answer comes back to the extension's identity.getRedirectURL() address with a one-time code the extension trades for its session, with PKCE (packages/sync/src/extension-sign-in.ts). A server that doesn't know the extension's return address gets a device code instead.
   >
   > Factwright on this computer: it can also save to the Factwright desktop app on the same computer, with no account. For that, it declares nativeMessaging as an optional permission, and requests it with permissions.request only when the user chooses "Factwright on this computer" in setup, fills a form while set up that way, or, filling a form through sync, chooses "Use Factwright on this computer" in the sidebar (src/setup.ts, src/computer.ts, src/fill-way.ts). It talks only to com.factwright.save, a helper the Factwright desktop app installs and registers in ~/Library/Application Support/Mozilla/NativeMessagingHosts, or in the registry under HKCU\Software\Mozilla\NativeMessagingHosts, allowing save@factwright.com alone. It sends each saved job as an object of six strings (url, site, company, role, pay, posting) and nothing else, and each ask as it fills a form as an object of two strings (type "ask" and request, src/computer.ts). The helper only writes a job into the app's data folder, and passes an ask only to the app while it's open (its source is apps/desktop/helper).
   >
   > Filling: the popup's Fill, the context menu item and the keyboard shortcut fill that tab's form in its page, from the background (src/in-place.ts), with scripting.executeScript of one function, formPage in src/page/form.ts, reading each visible field's question, options and limits, and never what's in it, and gets what to put in each field from the user's profile, work history, resumes and past answers: from the app on this computer, or, set up through sync, from the user's synced job hunt (below). A small note in the page, the extension's own frame.html, put there with scripting.executeScript where the job sites' script doesn't run (src/notes.ts), says how it went; its Details, or the popup's, opens the sidebar (sidebar_action), which shows that fill as it was. On the job sites whose terms forbid filling their forms, Fill opens the sidebar, with answers to copy. It fills only fields the user can see, never a password or payment details, never agrees to anything, never replaces a file the user attached, and never submits the form. A box that suggests places as the user types gets their city typed in, as they would, and the matching suggestion picked with a click, a form's own lists are opened and picked from as a click would, and a form's Yes and No buttons are clicked as they would. An application that runs across several pages in one tab is filled a page at a time, each only when the user chooses: the user presses the form's own button to go on, never the extension, and the sidebar offers to fill the next page. There, a part for a job or school that the site filled with another one is left as it is, and the form's own button that adds a part for a job or school is pressed only when the user chooses that in the sidebar. A page to sign in or make an account gets no Fill button, and its password is never filled. When the user asks the sidebar for drafts, or a question's menu on the job application sites, it sends the app the form's own questions that nothing on file answers, and the app has the user's own AI assistant draft them, which the sidebar fills in once they're done, and each question's menu offers to pick. The popup's Mark as applied, on other sites, gives the app the user's answers to the form's own questions that Fill filled in that tab, where it's still on the page, and marks the job applied; its note on the page has Undo. It never fills the forms of the job sites whose terms forbid it (COPY_ONLY_SITES in src/applicant-systems.ts), and only gives answers to copy there.
   >
   > Filling through sync: set up to save through sync, on a computer without the desktop app, or with it closed, the sidebar and the job application sites' menus answer a form themselves (src/fill-way.ts, src/sync-fill.ts, src/synced.ts), with the account keys the extension already holds. The first fill reads every record's latest version from the user's sync server, and each fill after only what changed since, opening each with those keys and checking it as the app's sync does. It downloads only the files a fill needs: the profile, the work history, the job's own job.md, the job.md of each job folder the job tracker's rows don't name, as from an earlier desktop app, and the job's resume, cover letter, drafts and the PDFs it uploads. What it opens stays in the memory of the sidebar's page or the background, goes 5 minutes after its last use, and is never stored. The user's street, postal code and equal-opportunity answers never sync, so those are left for the user. When the user submits the form, by their own click on its submit button, or a page's own button to go on, or Enter in one of its boxes, it reads what the form's fields hold, never a password, and gives it to the background (src/applied.ts), which keeps only the answers to the form's own questions, in memory, and once the page says the application went in, or moves on with no errors showing, gives them to the app, or the user's synced job hunt, and marks the job applied, with a note in the page that offers Undo. On a page that shows such a form in a frame, the sidebar reads and fills the form in that frame, by its frame ID. The script never runs on the job sites whose terms forbid filling their forms.

To try saving or filling through sync, a Factwright account with sync turned on in the desktop app is needed, which approves linking the extension: ask in the review's thread for a test account. ### Data collection in 0.3.0
Chosen on October 24, 2026: 0.3.0 adds `personallyIdentifyingInfo` to `required`, beside 0.2.0's browsing activity, website content and authentication information (choice B, below).
- **What people see:** Firefox's install prompt, the listing and `about:addons` say "The developer says this extension collects: browsing activity, website content, authentication information, personally identifying information".
- **On the update:** everyone with 0.2.0 gets a prompt when 0.3.0 reaches them, "Factwright requires new settings to update", with "The developer says the extension will collect: personally identifying information". Firefox keeps 0.2.0 until they accept: `updatePromptHandler` in Firefox's `AddonManager.sys.mjs` holds an update that adds a required data type, as it does one that adds a permission.

Why: Mozilla's policies count as collection "any data collected, used, transferred, shared, or handled outside the add-on or the local browser", so Factwright on the same computer counts, as 0.2.0's declaration already took it. What 0.3.0 hands on that 0.2.0 didn't:
- **To Factwright on the same computer:** a form's questions as it fills it, which website content covers. The user's answers to a form's own questions, when they mark a job applied, or submit its application on a job application site they turned on, once the page says it went in. And a new answer they choose to keep in Profile, from the panel or a field's menu: a phone number, an email address, a link, their street, city or postal code, or one of their equal-opportunity answers (gender, ethnicity, race, veteran status or disability).
- **To their own sync account, end-to-end encrypted, filling through sync:** a form's new questions, which they leave with the job for their Claude, their answers to a form's own questions, when they mark it applied, or submit it on a job application site they turned on, and then a new answer they choose to keep in Profile, but never their street, postal code or equal-opportunity answers, which never sync. What it reads back from there to fill a form, it opens in the browser's memory alone.
- **Into the page they're filling, at their click:** their contact details, work history and education from Profile and their resumes, and, from Factwright on the same computer, the equal-opportunity answers they set in Profile. The page's own code can read what's filled before they submit it, and a location box's code sends what's typed there to its site, to look up places.

Mozilla's examples for `personallyIdentifyingInfo` are "contact information such as name and address, email, and phone number, as well as other identifying data such as ID numbers, voice or video recordings, age, demographic information, or biometric data", so each of these carries it. It matches the Chrome answers above and the privacy policy. And it's declared with 0.3.0, while the update prompt reaches only the people who have had 0.2.0 since Firefox listed it on October 19: a later version that added it would ask everyone who has the extension then.

The choices weighed:
- **A. Leave it as it was.** The install prompt and the listing would stay the same, and the update would ask no one anything. But they'd leave out the contact details and equal-opportunity answers the extension hands the app and puts in forms, which Mozilla's definition counts. A reviewer could turn 0.3.0 down for it, or Mozilla could ask for it later, when more people have the extension to ask.
- **B. Add `personallyIdentifyingInfo` to `required`:** chosen, as above.
- **C. B, and `healthInfo` too.** Mozilla's examples for it are "medical history, symptoms, diagnoses, treatments, procedures, or heart rate data". The one answer near it is a disability answer on an equal-opportunity form, which Fill picks only as the user set it in Profile, and the panel keeps only if they choose Save. B reads it as a demographic self-identification, which personally identifying information covers. C would read it as health information too, and the prompts would say "health information" to everyone, for an answer most people never set. If that reading changes, add `healthInfo` here, and tick Health information in Chrome's answers too.
- **D. Make `personallyIdentifyingInfo` optional.** Nothing would change at install or in the update. The extension would ask for it the first time the user fills a form, with `permissions.request({ data_collection: ['personallyIdentifyingInfo'] })` from the popup's or the sidebar's click, since a click in a page's frame can't ask, and fill no contact details, nor keep a new answer, without it. That's new code for Firefox alone, and a second prompt. `about:addons` would then list it as "Share personally identifying information with extension developer", which isn't where it goes.

## Once it's live
Each store's listing address is in `STORES` in [apps/site/src/stores.ts](../../site/src/stores.ts), with `live: false` until the store approves it. Set the store's `live` to true, and once the site is deployed, factwright.com/extension links to its listing, where its button said coming soon. The first store to go live also takes "coming soon" off the site's other mentions of the extension.

## Each update
Raise `version` in `apps/extension/package.json`, build, and upload the new zip to each store (with a new source zip for Firefox). Note what changed in the CHANGELOG. Paste the listing and the answers above again where they changed since the last upload: for 0.3.0, all of them.

0.3.0, the version that fills forms, asks nothing of people with 0.2.0 in Chrome. `sidePanel` and `declarativeContent` are Chrome's alone, and show no warning. The job application sites are optional host permissions, which the browser asks for only when someone turns them on, and `frame.html` is a web-accessible resource, which asks nothing at an update. Chrome 127 to 129 keep 0.2.0, as 0.3.0 needs Chrome 130. In Firefox, those ask nothing either, but 0.3.0 adds personally identifying information to the data it declares, so Firefox asks everyone with 0.2.0 first, and keeps 0.2.0 until they accept ([Data collection in 0.3.0](#data-collection-in-030)). A later version that adds a required data type asks the same of everyone who has the extension then.

A new optional permission, such as `https://*/*` for servers of your own or `nativeMessaging` for Factwright on this computer, asks nothing of people when the update reaches them: the browser asks only when they choose to use it. A new required one with a warning would ask everyone, and Chrome turns the extension off until they accept. `identity`, new in 0.2.0, is required, since Firefox can't take it as optional, and shows no warning in either browser, so the update asks nothing. A managed Chrome whose administrator blocks the `identity` permission (`ExtensionSettings`' `blocked_permissions`) won't load the extension.
