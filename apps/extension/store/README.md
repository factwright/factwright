# Publishing Save to Factwright

What the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons ask for, ready to paste, and the steps. Build first: `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build` makes `dist/factwright-chrome-<version>.zip` and `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The screenshots are in `screenshots/`, drawn by `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension e2e -- --screenshots`.

Before the first upload, the sync server and factwright.com must be deployed with this version: the server takes the extension's sign-in, and the privacy policy describes it. The same goes for the first upload that lets you choose a server of your own: factwright.com's privacy policy says it may reach one. And for the first upload that saves to Factwright on the same computer, the Factwright release that carries its helper comes out first, and factwright.com's privacy policy describes it: without that release, setup finds no Factwright to save to.

Version 0.2.0 signs in through the browser's own window wherever the server knows the stores' copies. With Factwright's sync it signs in with a code where the server doesn't, so it can go out before or after the sync server that knows them: until that server is deployed, it signs in with a code, as 0.1.x does. A server of your own signs it in through the window alone, never with a code, so a server of your own needs a version with the window. sync-server has published one with each release since Factwright 0.9.0, so every one deployed from there has it.

## The listing

**Name:** Save to Factwright

**Summary** (both stores, under 132 characters):
> Save the job you're looking at, with its posting, to your Factwright job hunt. End-to-end encrypted.

**Description:**
> Save to Factwright adds the job you're looking at to your Factwright job hunt, with its link and its posting as you saw it.
>
> On a job's page, click the Factwright button, check the title, company and posting, and save it. Or press Alt+Shift+S, or right-click the page, to save it without opening anything. It reads the job details a page publishes, the layout of the job boards and applicant tracking systems people use most, or the text you selected, so it also saves postings you can only see when signed in. The job shows in Factwright on your computer as New, ready for a fit check, a tailored resume or a cover letter, with the posting kept even after the listing comes down.
>
> Private by design:
> • It reads a page only when you click its button, its menu item or its shortcut, and nothing as you browse. The popup sends nothing until you save.
> • Saved to Factwright on the same computer, a job goes straight to the app, never to a server. Through sync, what it saves is locked on your computer with your job hunt's own key before it's sent, so the sync server stores it in a form it can't read.
> • It reaches no site but your sync server, Factwright's or one you run yourself, and has no analytics or tracking.
> • A setting lets it save LinkedIn jobs by their link alone, without reading their postings.
>
> Save to Factwright costs nothing to add. It needs Factwright, the free desktop app for Mac and Windows: factwright.com. It saves straight to Factwright on the same computer, with no account, or through sync from any computer, with Factwright's sync or a server of your own. Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync. Factwright is open source: github.com/factwright/factwright

Both stores ask a listing to say when any of what an extension does needs something paid, so both carry the price: "Factwright's sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, and a server of your own is free: factwright.com/sync." If the price changes, change it here, on factwright.com and in both listings together.

Firefox Add-ons reads the description as Markdown, and an edit there goes live as soon as it's saved. Paste it with a blank line after "Private by design:", and each point starting with "- " instead of "• ". Chrome's description is plain text, as above, and a change to it waits for the store's review.

Don't list other companies' names in it. The Chrome Web Store rejected the first submission for keyword spam (violation Yellow Argon) over "LinkedIn, Indeed, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and other job sites". One mention of LinkedIn, for its setting, is the most it should carry.

**Category:** Chrome: Productivity (Workflow & Planning). Firefox: Productivity.

**Homepage:** https://factwright.com. **Support:** https://github.com/factwright/factwright/issues. **Privacy policy:** https://factwright.com/privacy#extension.

**Screenshots** (1280×800, in this order):
1. `screenshots/1-save.png`: Check it, save it.
2. `screenshots/2-saved.png`: Saved as New, with the posting as you saw it.
3. `screenshots/3-link.png`: Linked by Factwright on your computer, once the emoji match.
4. `screenshots/4-settings.png`: Save it your way, and choose how it treats LinkedIn.

## Chrome Web Store
1. Sign in at https://chrome.google.com/webstore/devconsole with the Google account that should own the listing, pay the one-time $5 registration, and verify the contact email. Choose whether the publisher shows as a trader (Neatifai LLC) or not. Turn on 2-Step Verification for that Google account first: the extension holds its users' keys, so whoever can publish it could read their job hunts ([docs/security.md](../../../docs/security.md#the-browser-extension)).
2. **New item**, then upload `dist/factwright-chrome-<version>.zip`.
3. **Store listing:** the name, summary, description, category, screenshots, homepage and support links above. The icon comes from the zip.
4. **Privacy:**
   - **Single purpose:** Helps the user apply to jobs with their own Factwright job hunt: it saves the job posting in the current tab to it, to the Factwright desktop app on the same computer or through Factwright's end-to-end encrypted sync, and, with the desktop app on the same computer, fills in the job's application form in that tab for the user to check and submit themselves.
   - **activeTab:** To read the job on the tab the user is on, only when they click the extension's button, its menu items or its shortcuts. The popup shows what it read and sends nothing until the user saves. When the user clicks Fill this application, the same grant lets it read that tab's application form, fill it, and, while its side panel shows that form, read what's in the form's fields, until the tab leaves the page.
   - **scripting:** To run the one function that reads the job's title, company and posting in that tab, when the user clicks the extension's button, its menu item or its shortcut, and the one that reads and fills the tab's application form when they click Fill this application.
   - **storage:** To keep the user's settings, such as whether to read LinkedIn postings.
   - **contextMenus:** For the Save job to Factwright and Fill this application items in the page's right-click menu.
   - **sidePanel:** For the panel beside the page where the user checks how the extension filled an application form: each field with its answer and where it came from. It opens only when the user clicks Fill this application.
   - **declarativeContent:** To light the toolbar button up on pages that look like an application form, by rules Chrome checks itself (a file upload with an email address, or a file upload on an applicant system's site), so the extension reads nothing to do it.
   - **identity:** To sign the user in to their Factwright sync account with chrome.identity.launchWebAuthFlow, when they click Sign in to sync in setup. Chrome's own sign-in window opens the sync server's page (app.factwright.com, or the user's own server), where they sign in and allow the extension, and Chrome hands the answer to this extension alone, so there's no code to copy or compare. It never reads the user's Google account or email (no identity.email or getAuthToken).
   - **nativeMessaging** (optional): Only for saving to the Factwright desktop app on the same computer, with no account, and for filling application forms from it. Nothing is asked at install: when the user chooses "Factwright on this computer" in setup, or clicks Fill this application, the extension asks for it with permissions.request. It then sends each job the user saves, and nothing else (its title, company, pay, link, the site it's on, and its posting text), and as it fills a form, the form's questions, never what's in them, to Factwright's own helper, com.factwright.save, which the Factwright app installs and registers for this extension's ID alone. The app answers with what to put in each field, from what the user told it. When the user marks a job applied, it sends their answers to the form's own questions, to keep with the job. It gives the permission back if the user sets it up to save through sync instead.
   - **Host permission** (one box covers both):
     > app.factwright.com: To save the job to the user's job hunt through Factwright's sync server, the one site the extension reaches unless the user chooses a sync server of their own.
     >
     > https://*/* (optional): Only for people who run their own Factwright sync server, which is open source. Nothing is asked at install. When the user chooses "Your own server" in setup and types its https address, the extension asks the browser for that one address alone (permissions.request with that origin), checks it answers as a Factwright sync server, and saves through it instead of app.factwright.com. It never asks for any other site, and gives the permission back when the user signs out, or when signing in there fails or is canceled.
   - **Remote code:** No, I am not using remote code.
   - **Data usage,** collected only when the user saves a job, and end-to-end encrypted to their own account, or handed to the desktop app on the same computer: **Website content** (the posting's text, and an application form's questions), **Web history** (the address of the page they saved or filled), **Authentication information** (the extension's own sync sign-in), and **Personally identifiable information** (the user's own details, from the desktop app on the same computer, which the extension puts in an application form when they click Fill this application, and their answers to a form's questions, which it gives the app when they mark a job applied).
   - **Certify** all three: not sold or transferred to third parties, not used for purposes unrelated to the single purpose, and not used for creditworthiness or lending.
   - **Privacy policy URL:** https://factwright.com/privacy#extension
5. **Distribution:** public, all regions.
6. **Submit for review.** Reviews usually take a few days. A first listing that asks for host permissions can take longer, and so can the first version with the optional `https://*/*`, for servers of your own: the console may flag it as broad host permissions and send it for an in-depth review. The first version with the optional `nativeMessaging` may take longer too.

## Firefox Add-ons
1. Sign in at https://addons.mozilla.org/developers/ with a Mozilla account, turn on its two-step authentication, and accept the developer agreement.
2. **Submit a New Add-on,** on this site (listed), then upload `dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The manifest's ID is `save@factwright.com`, and it needs Firefox 140 or later, the first to show what an extension sends at install. Firefox has taken the optional host permission for servers of your own since 128, and native messaging as an optional permission since 87.
3. **Source code:** yes, the code is bundled. Upload a zip of the repository at the commit the zip was built from (`git archive --format=zip -o factwright-source.zip HEAD`), and paste these steps:
   > Node.js 24 and pnpm 12.10.1 (`corepack enable`). From the repository's root: `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, then `pnpm --filter @factwright/extension build`. The add-on is `apps/extension/dist/firefox`, and its zip is `apps/extension/dist/factwright-firefox-<version>.zip`. The code isn't minified.
4. **Listing:** the name, summary, description, categories, screenshots, homepage, support and privacy policy above. Under the license, AGPL-3.0-only. Once Factwright sells sync, a version from before servers of your own, which can save only through Factwright's sync, also needs `requires_payment` marked ("requires payment, non-free services or software"). From 0.2.0, the version with servers of your own, it doesn't, and that's the version Firefox has listed since October 19, 2026.
5. **Data collection:** the manifest declares what it sends, which Firefox shows at install: browsing activity (the address of a job the user saves), website content (its posting) and authentication information (its sync sign-in). Filling a form puts the user's own details, from the desktop app on the same computer, in the page, and gives the app the user's answers when they mark a job applied, all on the same computer. Before uploading the version that fills forms, check whether Mozilla counts that as personally identifying information sent, and if so, add `personallyIdentifyingInfo` to the manifest's `data_collection_permissions`.
6. **Notes to reviewer:**
   > Save to Factwright adds the job in the current tab to the user's Factwright job hunt, end-to-end encrypted, through Factwright's own sync server (app.factwright.com, its one host permission at install), or through a Factwright sync server the user runs themselves. For that, it declares the optional host permission https://*/*: only when the user chooses "Your own server" in setup and types its https address does it request that one origin with permissions.request (src/setup.ts, src/account.ts). It then checks the server answers /api/sign-in-options as a Factwright sync server before signing in, sends nothing to any other site, and removes the permission when the user signs out, or the sign-in fails or is canceled. It signs in with identity.launchWebAuthFlow (src/account.ts): Firefox's window opens the sync server's own page, where the user allows it, and the server's answer comes back to the extension's identity.getRedirectURL() address with a one-time code the extension trades for its session, with PKCE (packages/sync/src/extension-sign-in.ts). A server that doesn't know the extension's return address gets a device code instead. It reads a tab only when the user clicks its button, its context menu item or its keyboard shortcut (activeTab with scripting.executeScript of one function, readJobPage in src/page/read-job.ts, which reads the page without changing it). The popup sends nothing until the user saves. It has no content scripts, no web-accessible resources and no remote code. It can also save to the Factwright desktop app on the same computer, with no account: for that, it declares nativeMessaging as an optional permission, and requests it with permissions.request only when the user chooses "Factwright on this computer" in setup (src/setup.ts, src/computer.ts). It then sends each saved job, as an object of six strings (url, site, company, role, pay, posting) and nothing else, to com.factwright.save, a helper the Factwright desktop app installs and registers in ~/Library/Application Support/Mozilla/NativeMessagingHosts (Mac) or HKCU\Software\Mozilla\NativeMessagingHosts (Windows), allowing save@factwright.com alone. The helper only writes the job into the app's data folder (its source is apps/desktop/helper). With the desktop app open, it also fills in a job's application form for the user to check, when they click Fill this application: the popup, the context menu item and the keyboard shortcut open its sidebar (sidebar_action), which reads that tab's form with scripting.executeScript of one function, formPage in src/page/form.ts, reading each visible field's question and never what's in it, and asks the app, through the same helper, what to put in each field (an object of two strings, type "ask" and request, src/computer.ts). It fills only fields the user can see, never a password, and never submits the form. While the sidebar shows the form, it reads what's in the form's own questions every few seconds, so that when the user marks the job applied it can give the app their answers to them. It never fills LinkedIn's or Indeed's forms, and only gives answers to copy there. Saving through sync needs a Factwright account with sync turned on in the desktop app (factwright.com), which approves linking the extension. Factwright's sync is a subscription, so we can give a reviewer's account sync for free: ask in the review's thread. The source is public at https://github.com/factwright/factwright, under apps/extension.

   Reviewers can try it with no account: install Factwright from factwright.com and open it (on a Mac, then choose Browser extension in its sidebar and Set up beside Firefox), then choose Factwright on this computer in the extension's setup. To try saving through sync, they need a Factwright account with sync on and the desktop app to link it, so a test account may be asked for: answer in the review's thread. Once sync is sold, give the reviewer's account free sync, as [apps/server's README](../../server/README.md#giving-sync-away) says.

## Once it's live
Each store's listing address is in `STORES` in [apps/site/src/stores.ts](../../site/src/stores.ts), with `live: false` until the store approves it. Set the store's `live` to true, and once the site is deployed, factwright.com/extension links to its listing, where its button said coming soon. The first store to go live also takes "coming soon" off the site's other mentions of the extension.

## Each update
Raise `version` in `apps/extension/package.json`, build, and upload the new zip to each store (with a new source zip for Firefox). Note what changed in the CHANGELOG. Paste the listing and the answers above again where they changed since the last upload.

`sidePanel` and `declarativeContent`, new in the version that fills forms, are Chrome's alone, and show no warning, so that update asks nothing either. A new optional permission, such as `https://*/*` for servers of your own or `nativeMessaging` for Factwright on this computer, asks nothing of people when the update reaches them: the browser asks only when they choose to use it. A new required one with a warning would ask everyone, and Chrome turns the extension off until they accept. `identity`, new in 0.2.0, is required, since Firefox can't take it as optional, and shows no warning in either browser, so the update asks nothing. A managed Chrome whose administrator blocks the `identity` permission (`ExtensionSettings`' `blocked_permissions`) won't load the extension.
