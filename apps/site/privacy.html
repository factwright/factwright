The short version

  • Your job hunt lives on your computer. Unless you turn on sync, the app sends us nothing from it, and it has no analytics, ads or crash reports.
  • Your own Claude does the work, under your own Anthropic account. What it reads goes to Anthropic, not to us, and we never see your Claude login.
  • Sync is optional. If you turn it on, your job hunt is encrypted on your computer before it’s sent, with a key we never see, so our servers store it only in a form they can’t read. The web view on your phone works differently, as Sync and the web view explains.
  • The browser extension reads a page only when you ask it to, or, on the job application sites you turn on, the application forms on their pages. It fills applications from the app on your computer, or, through sync, from your job hunt, which it opens in your browser alone.
  • We keep only what sync needs: your name, email and account ID from the account you sign in with, the devices you sign in on, and your encrypted job hunt with its sizes and times.
  • If you pay for sync, Stripe takes the payment, and we never see your full card number. Our server keeps only Stripe’s IDs for you and your subscription, with its status and dates, and our Stripe account keeps your payments for our records.
  • We never sell your data or share it for advertising. You can download or delete it whenever you like.

Who we are

Factwright is made by Neatifai LLC, a company in Illinois, in the United States. In this policy, “we” and “us” mean Neatifai LLC. This policy covers the Factwright app for Mac and Windows, its browser extension for Chrome and Firefox, this website, the app’s downloads and updates, and the optional sync service and web view at app.factwright.com.

For anything about your privacy, email privacy@factwright.com.

On your computer

The app keeps your job hunt in a folder on your computer: your fact bank, resumes, documents and notes, plus a small database that tracks your jobs. Files you add, such as old resumes or your LinkedIn profile saved as a PDF, are copied into that folder. None of it is sent to us unless you turn on sync, below. Until then, the app’s only contact with us is checking for updates.

The app has no analytics, ads or tracking, and sends no crash reports or usage data. On Windows, its spell checker downloads a dictionary from Google once; nothing you type is sent.

Uninstalling Factwright leaves its data folder where it is: your job hunt, its tracker and sync’s settings. To remove them, delete the Factwright folder in ~/Library/Application Support on a Mac, or in %APPDATA% on Windows.

Your Claude

Factwright starts the Claude Code you installed, signed in to your own Anthropic account. When Claude works, it sends what it reads, such as your files, job postings and your answers, to Anthropic under your account. Claude Code also sends Anthropic its own usage and error reports, as Anthropic’s policies and your settings allow, and keeps a record of each run on your computer. Anthropic’s terms, its privacy policy and your own Claude settings, such as whether your conversations can be used to improve its models, apply to all of that.

We never see, store or pass on your Claude login, and we’re not part of that connection. Factwright isn’t made by Anthropic or affiliated with it.

Web pages and job boards

When you or your Claude look for jobs, the app reads job boards on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Gem, Hacker News’s “Who is hiring?” threads through Algolia’s Hacker News search, and other web pages, straight from your computer. Those sites see your computer’s IP address, as with any visit, and that Factwright is asking. Claude’s web searches go to Anthropic, under your account.

For a job saved from a posting on Greenhouse, Get the form’s questions asks Greenhouse’s public job API for that job’s application questions, straight from your computer, only when you click it. It sends only the board’s name and the job’s ID, from the posting’s link, and Greenhouse sees your IP address, as with any visit. Factwright keeps the questions with the job, in your job hunt, for your Claude to draft.

Factwright never signs in to LinkedIn, or to any job site, for you.

Updates

While the app is open, it checks downloads.factwright.com for a new version every 10 minutes, and when you come back to it or wake your computer. Each check shows our hosting provider, Cloudflare, your IP address and that a copy of Factwright is checking. It carries no ID for you or your computer.

Our websites

factwright.com sets no cookies, has no ads, and loads nothing from other sites. The one thing it counts is downloads, so we can see how many people get Factwright: when you download it from the site, we count which computer it’s for, which version and when, and keep nothing about you, not even your IP address. We keep each count for three months. Cloudflare runs it, our downloads and the sync service. It handles each request, including your IP address and browser, to deliver it and keep it secure, and gives us traffic statistics for up to 30 days. Cloudflare may also set a cookie it needs to tell people from bots.

Sync and the web view

Sync is off until you turn it on. It lets your other computers and your phone show the same job hunt, in the app or in the web view at app.factwright.com. To use it, you sign in with GitHub, or with Google where it’s offered.

What we can read

  • From the account you sign in with: your name, email address and that account’s ID. GitHub or Google also sends a token that could read that profile again. We don’t keep it.
  • For each device you sign in on: its name, such as “Safari on iPhone”, or for the app, your computer’s name, and when it signed in and was last used.
  • For what you sync: each item’s size and times, whether it’s deleted, and which of your devices changed it, under IDs that say nothing about what’s in it.
  • Your IP address with each recent request to sign in or check your sign-in, counted to slow down attacks.

What we can’t read

Your job hunt itself: your files and their names, your facts, resumes and notes, and everything you track. Your computer encrypts it before it’s uploaded, with a key we never see, so our servers store it only in a form they can’t read. We hold that key only locked: with your recovery key, which we never receive, and with a link code when you add a device. A link code works for 10 minutes, and its locked copy is deleted once it’s used or cancelled, or within a day.

The web view works differently. It opens your job hunt in your browser with code our server sends each time you open it. Anyone who controlled that server could change that code to read everything you’ve synced, in any browser you’ve linked, and your recovery key if you type it there. The desktop app’s code changes only through updates we sign.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and your encrypted job hunt: until you delete your account.
  • Earlier versions of what you change: 90 days.
  • Daily backups: 14 days.
  • Each device’s sign-in: until you sign it out, or within a day of it running out, after 60 days without use.
  • Counts of requests from your IP address: about a day.
  • What our servers note for themselves, such as a request that failed with its address, the account ID when we delete an account someone asked us to, or Stripe’s IDs for a payment that needs a look, but nothing from your job hunt: up to 7 days, at Cloudflare.

Deleting your account removes your account, your job hunt, your backups and your sign-ins straight away. It needs a sign-in with GitHub or Google in the last 15 minutes. Anything we delete can stay in Cloudflare’s own recovery systems for up to 30 days more.

The web view

The web view uses cookies only to sign you in and keep you signed in, and keeps your keys in your browser’s storage so it can open your job hunt there. It has no analytics or tracking. Signing out forgets the keys in that browser, and so does finding it’s been signed out another way. Your browser may also keep locked copies of files in its cache, and files you download or open there are saved by your browser as they are, unlocked.

Paying for sync

Sync through our server is a subscription. Link, Stripe’s checkout, takes your payment, with Stripe’s affiliate Sold through Link, LLC, as the merchant of record, so what you give at checkout, such as your name, card and billing address, goes to Stripe, which handles it under its own privacy policy. We never see your full card number.

When you first go to subscribe, we give Stripe your account’s email address and ID, so your subscription stays with your account. On our server, we keep:

  • Stripe’s IDs for you as its customer, and for your subscription
  • your subscription’s status, whether you’ve canceled it, and when the time you’ve paid for ends
  • when we last checked it with Stripe, when you last started a checkout, and when any of this last changed
  • whether we gave you sync for free because you asked

We keep these until you delete your account. Deleting it deletes your customer record at Stripe too, which ends your subscription at once.

If we give free sync to an email address with no account yet, we keep that address, and when we gave it, until an account signs up with it, or until you ask us to forget it.

Stripe also shows us, in our Stripe account, your name, email address and billing address, the kind of card you used and its last four digits, and your payments and any refunds. We use them only to run your subscription, to help with a payment, and for the records tax law requires. Your payments stay there, and in Stripe’s own records, for as long as that law requires, even after you delete your account.

The browser extension

Factwright’s extension for Chrome and Firefox saves a job you’re looking at to your job hunt, and fills in a job’s application form for you to check, from the Factwright app on the same computer or from your job hunt through sync. It has no analytics or tracking, and it never submits a form. Our use of what it reads complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Saving a job

It reads a page only when you click its button, its menu item or its shortcut, and sends nothing until you save. It reads the job’s address, its title, company, place and pay, and the posting’s text, or the text you selected. On a site it doesn’t know, the posting it reads is the page’s main text, which it shows you to check before you save it. A LinkedIn job is read the same way, when you click to save it, unless you turn that off in its settings, which then saves the link and title alone.

Saved to Factwright on the same computer, a job goes from your browser to the app through the browser’s own messaging, by way of a small helper the app installs, which your browser lets only our extension reach. It goes to no server on the way, and it leaves your computer only if you sync your job hunt, which locks it first, as with everything you sync. Saving this way, the extension keeps no sign-in and no keys.

Through sync, what it saves is locked on your computer with your job hunt’s key before it’s sent, so we store it only in a form we can’t read, as with everything you sync. The extension keeps its own sign-in to sync, and your job hunt’s keys, in your browser until you sign it out. Those keys open your whole job hunt, as they do on each device you link, so the extension keeps them where they can’t be copied out, and uses them only to lock what it saves, to open the job already at the ID it would save to, which may be one you added in the app, to tell whether it’s the same job, and to read your job hunt when you fill a form through sync, below. It reaches no site but app.factwright.com, or a sync server you run yourself, if you choose one in its setup.

Filling an application

When you click Fill this application, it reads the application form on that page: each field you can see, with its question, its options and its limits, and not what’s in it. With the Factwright app on the same computer, it asks the app for an answer to each, through the same helper, and the app answers from what you’ve told it: Profile, your work history and resumes, what you sent before, and your Claude’s drafts. Without it, the extension answers from your job hunt through sync, below. It puts the answers in the form for you to check, and its panel lists each one with where it came from. It fills only fields you can see, never a password or payment details, never answers personal questions, such as your age, which it never keeps either, and never agrees to anything for you. With the app, all of this stays on your computer, between your browser and the app. On LinkedIn and Indeed it only gives you answers to copy.

It fills an application that runs over several pages a page at a time, each only when you choose, as you go on with the form’s own button: it never presses one that goes on or submits. There, it leaves a field that already holds an answer as it is, as some sites fill one in from your resume, and adds a part to the form for each job or school you have on file only if you choose that. It keeps what you sent on each page for when you mark the job applied. A page to sign in or make an account gets nothing from it, and never your password.

What it fills reaches the site when you submit the form, as what you type does, and the page’s own code can read a field before that. Some boxes look up places as you type, such as where you live: there, the page’s code sends what the extension types to its site, to find matching places, as it would what you type.

While its panel shows the form, it reads what’s in the form’s own questions, and in the fields Profile keeps, so it has what you sent once you submit it on the site. When you mark the job applied, it gives the app your answers to the form’s own questions, to keep with the job, and offers to keep each new answer you gave a field Profile keeps, such as a new phone number, which it does only if you choose Save. Your equal-opportunity answers, street and postal code stay in the app’s own folder on your computer: they never sync, and Claude never sees them.

For the questions nothing on file answers, the panel offers Draft them with your Claude. If you click it, the extension gives the app those questions, as the form asks them, and never what’s in them. The app keeps them with the job, in your job hunt, and starts your Claude on them, so what Claude reads goes to Anthropic under your account, as Your Claude says. The panel fills in each draft once it’s done.

Filling through sync

In a browser set up to save through sync, on a computer without the Factwright app, or with it closed, the extension answers a form itself, from your job hunt, as the app would. It reads what sync holds from the sync server and opens it with your job hunt’s keys, in your browser: the job tracker, Profile, your work history, and only the files a form needs, such as the job’s resume, cover letter and your Claude’s drafts. What it opens stays in your browser’s memory for the fill, goes 5 minutes after its last use, and is never stored. Your street, postal code and equal-opportunity answers never sync, so it leaves those for you.

Instead of starting your Claude, the panel offers to leave a form’s new questions with the job, for your Claude to draft in the app, and fills in the drafts at your next Fill, once they’ve synced. When you mark the job applied, it keeps what you sent with the job. Each of these is locked in your browser before it’s sent, as everything you sync is. Through sync, it never keeps a new answer in Profile: that needs the app.

On the job application sites you allow

The extension runs no script of its own in pages unless you turn on the job application sites, in its setup or its panel: one permission for the sites where the applicant systems most companies use show their jobs and forms, and any other careers site you add, one at a time. Your browser asks you first. It’s never every site, and never LinkedIn or Indeed. Turning them off, in the extension or in your browser’s settings, takes its script away.

On those sites, and only there, its script reads each field you can see, with its question and options, to put a small Fill button on a page with an application form. The first time you click or tab into one of the form’s fields, the extension asks the app on your computer for the form’s answers, with its fields and never what’s in them, or reads them from your job hunt through sync, and a field you go to shows a menu of its answers on file. The menu is the extension’s own, and the page can’t read it: the page gets only the answer you pick, which the script puts in the field only then. With the app on your computer, the script reads what’s in a field only once you’ve typed in it and left it, and only for a field whose answer Profile keeps, to offer to keep a new answer, which it does only if you choose Save. Through sync, it reads none. It never reads a password or an equal-opportunity answer. Those sites can tell the extension is there, as its button and menus are on their pages, and whether Factwright has an answer for a field, as a menu shows, but not what the answer is.

Forms in another site’s page

A company’s careers page may show an application form in a frame from an applicant system’s site. Where that’s one of the sites you allowed, the extension’s script runs in the frame, never in the page around it, and Fill this application reads and fills the form there. What it reads in the frame goes to the extension, never to the page around it, which can’t see into the frame, nor into the extension’s menus. To find the form again after the extension restarts in the background, it keeps which frames its script is in while your browser runs, and never on your disk.

Emails

If you email us, we use what you send to answer you. We keep it as long as we need to answer you, then up to 2 years for our records. If you ask for free sync, we also use your email address to turn it on, as Paying for sync explains.

Supporting Factwright

If you support Factwright through Buy Me a Coffee or GitHub Sponsors, it goes to Steve Hillyer personally, who builds Factwright for us, and we don’t receive it. The service takes your payment and keeps its own records, under its own privacy policy, and may show your name and message on its pages unless you choose not to. Steve sees what it shares, such as your name or username, your email address, the amount and any message, and uses it only to thank you and for tax records. It’s separate from paying for sync, and none of it is linked to your Factwright account. You can ask about it at privacy@factwright.com.

Who we share it with

We don’t sell your personal information, or share it for advertising. Two companies handle it for us, and only for us:

  • Cloudflare runs this website, the downloads and updates, and the sync service, and stores what sync keeps. It also passes on emails you send us.
  • Google Workspace, our email provider, holds those emails.

GitHub, and Google if you use it, let you sign in to sync. They see that you signed in to Factwright, under their own privacy policies. Anthropic isn’t one of our providers either: your Claude account is your own.

If you pay for sync, Stripe gets your email address and account ID from us and what you give it at checkout, and keeps our records of your payments, both for us and under its own privacy policy, as Paying for sync explains.

We may disclose information if the law requires it, such as a valid court order, or to protect someone’s safety or our rights. Even then, we can hand over only what we have, and we can’t unlock your encrypted job hunt. If Neatifai or Factwright is ever sold or merged, your information would go with it, still under this policy.

Where it’s kept

We’re in the United States. Cloudflare runs our services on its network around the world, and Stripe takes payments around the world too, so your information may be stored or handled in the United States and other countries. Wherever we keep it, this policy applies.

Why we use it

We use your account and device details to run the sync you asked for, which is our agreement with you, and to keep the service working and secure, which is in your interest and ours. If you pay for sync, we use your payment details only to run your subscription, help with a payment and keep the records the law requires, which is our agreement with you and the law. Once a day, the sync service also counts how many accounts it has, how many are new and how many were used in the last week and month, and how many devices are signed in, in the app and in browsers. It keeps only those totals, for three months, so we can see how much it’s used, which is in our interest. If you email us, we use what you send to answer you. We don’t use your information for anything else, such as profiling or advertising.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can:

  • see what we keep about you, and get a copy
  • correct it
  • delete it, along with your account
  • object to how we use it, or ask us to limit it
  • ask us anything about it

Much of this is in the web view, where your account page lists your devices, downloads everything you’ve synced, and deletes your account. For anything else, including deleting an account you can’t sign in to, email privacy@factwright.com from your account’s email address. Someone you’ve authorized can also ask for you. We’ll answer within 30 days, and we’ll never treat you differently for asking. If we turn down a request, you can ask us to look at it again, and you can complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Security

Sync is encrypted on your computer, every connection uses HTTPS, each account’s data is kept apart from everyone else’s, and the app and its updates are signed. No system is perfectly secure, so if something ever happens that affects your information, we’ll tell you as soon as we can.

If you lose every device and your recovery key, your synced job hunt can’t be recovered, by you or by us. Your job hunt on your computer isn’t affected.

Age

Factwright is for people 18 and older. We don’t knowingly collect information from anyone younger. If you think someone under 18 has an account, tell us and we’ll delete it.

Do Not Track

We don’t track you across other sites, and we never sell your information or share it for advertising, so everyone is treated the same, whether or not their browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we’ll post the new version here with a new date. If a change matters, we’ll tell you in the app or by email before it takes effect.
