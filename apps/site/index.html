True to the facts.

A job hunt app for people putting their real experience forward. Your own Claude finds jobs that fit, tailors each resume and cover letter to the job from facts you’ve confirmed, and gets you ready for the interview.

What it does

  • Tailors every resume to the job, using only facts you’ve confirmed, then checks each line against them.
  • Runs on your own Claude, signed in as you, so runs count against your plan. Factwright never sees your login.
  • Keeps your job hunt on your computer. Turn on sync and it’s encrypted before it leaves, with a key we never see, so our servers store it only in a form they can’t read.

What it never does

  • Apply for you. Factwright drafts and you decide. You send every application yourself.
  • Make things up. No fact, no line. If a job wants something you haven’t done, it stays off your resume.
  • Help during an interview. It gets you ready beforehand. In the room, it’s all you.

How it works

Set it up once. Then every job is a few clicks.

Factwright does the busywork of a job hunt with your Claude: the reading, the tailoring, the checking and the tracking. You make the calls.

  1. 1

    Tell it about you

    Share your old resumes, your LinkedIn profile saved as a PDF, screenshots of your work, and everything you’d say about yourself in your own words, from projects outside work to what you do for fun. Then say what you’re after: titles, pay, where, the kinds of company, and your deal-breakers.

    About 20 minutes, saved as you go, so you can stop any time.

  2. 2

    Confirm your fact bank

    Your Claude turns all of it into a fact bank: your experience as short, checkable claims, each marked unconfirmed. Then it interviews you, a few questions at a time, to confirm each one and fill the gaps.

    Nothing goes on a resume until you’ve said it’s true.

  3. 3

    Find jobs that fit

    Point it at Hacker News’s monthly “Who is hiring?” thread, LinkedIn’s and Indeed’s listings, a company’s job board or a careers page, or paste any posting. Our browser extension, coming soon, saves the one you’re looking at. Every job gets scored against your facts, and a fit check adds what people say about working there. Then decide on each with a click: apply, later or pass.

    A salary check estimates pay a posting hides, and an unmask finds the company behind a recruiter’s post.

  4. 4

    Click what’s up next, and your Claude gets to work

    Every job says what to do next, with the button that does it, from Run a fit check and Tailor a resume to Prep for the call. Everything else has a place of its own in the job, with a button that makes the first, such as Write a cover letter or Answer the questions. Keep working while your own Claude Code does it: a bar along the bottom says what it’s doing, and Show Claude’s work opens it, to watch or type to.

    • WorkingThe bar says what Claude is doing, a step at a time.
    • Needs your answerClaude’s question opens in a box of its own.
    • DoneThe bar says what Claude made, with Open it.
  5. 5

    Apply, and keep track

    Resumes come out as PDF or Word in your templates, filling the pages you chose and fact-checked line by line. An ATS X-ray shows what an applicant tracking system reads in each one. You send them yourself. Then track every status, call and interview, and see what needs you next. Add a call to your calendar, and Factwright reminds you an hour before, while it’s open.

    Turn on sync to see it all on your phone.

Skills for your Claude

Ready for every part of the hunt.

Factwright gives your Claude a skill for each task, grounded in your facts. A job’s Up next offers the one to do now, and each has a place of its own in the job, with the button that starts it. What Claude makes shows up there, ready to read.

  • Find jobs

    Reads a hiring thread, job boards and careers pages, and ranks what fits. Give one search its own focus, such as early startups.

    Find jobs, at the top of your jobs

  • Fit check

    Scores a job against your facts, from strong to skip, with what people say about it.

    Up next, or Run a fit check in the job’s Fit

  • Unmask a listing

    Finds the real company behind a recruiter’s post, and the direct way to apply.

    Up next, as Find the real employer, for a recruiter’s post

  • Salary check

    Estimates the pay when a posting doesn’t say, with sources and how sure it is.

    Estimate the pay, in the job’s Fit

  • Tailored resume

    A resume for the job in your templates, with every line traced to a fact.

    Up next, or Tailor a resume in Resumes and cover letters

  • ATS X-ray

    Reads your resume the way an applicant tracking system does, and checks the job’s key terms. Claude fixes what you pick.

    ATS X-ray, on each resume in Resumes and cover letters

  • Cover letter

    A letter in your own voice, fact-checked like your resume and made to match it.

    Up next when a posting asks for one, or Write a cover letter

  • Projects page

    Your projects in depth, on one page, for the jobs where they help.

    Up next when a posting asks for samples of your work, or Make a Projects page

  • Application questions

    Answers to the form in your own voice, short and ready to paste.

    Answer the questions, in the job’s Application questions

  • Who to message

    Three to five people worth reaching, any warm path, and drafts you send yourself.

    Find people to message, in People to contact

  • Interview prep

    Likely questions answered from your facts, stories to tell, and questions to ask.

    Interview prep, in Calls and interviews, for the company as a whole

  • Call prep

    A prep sheet for each call that builds on the rounds before. Practice in a mock interview, then get thank-you notes to send.

    Up next before each call, from Prep for the call to Draft thank-you notes

Anything else about a job? Ask a question, in its Questions for Claude. Your Claude answers from everything on file and the web, and the answer stays with the job.

Templates

Two clean looks, or bring your own.

Start with Classic or Minimal. Or show Factwright a resume whose look you like, and your own Claude makes a template in that look. Whatever the look, the words come from your facts, and every resume fills the pages you chose, with no half-empty last page.

  • Classic

    Traditional and all serif, in black and white.

  • Minimal

    Quiet and airy, with gray headings and no color.

  • A PDF or a picture

    Your own

    Its layout, fonts, colors and spacing, never its words. Private, on your computer.

Every resume comes as a PDF or a Word file, with a cover letter to match when you want one. One-column looks read best in applicant systems.

Your Claude, your files

Your own Claude does the work, where you can see it.

Factwright doesn’t have an AI of its own. It starts the Claude Code you installed and signed in to, unmodified, and hands it Factwright’s skills for that run only.

  1. On your computer

    Factwright, your Claude Code, and your job hunt as plain files in a folder you own. Claude works only inside that folder, with no command line.

  2. With Anthropic, as you

    Claude Code talks to Anthropic under your own account, so runs count against your Claude plan or API key. Factwright never sees, stores or passes your login.

  3. Synced, if you want it

    Turn on sync to see your hunt on your other computers and your phone. It’s encrypted before it leaves your computer, with a key we never see, so our servers store it only in a form they can’t read. It stays off until you turn it on, and it’s $5 a month on ours, or free on a sync server of your own.

  • Asks before it browses

    Postings are untrusted text, so by default Claude asks before it opens a web page. Boards on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Gem are read without asking.

  • Never signs in to LinkedIn

    Share LinkedIn’s own PDF or data export instead, and your account stays out of it.

  • You send everything

    Factwright drafts and you decide. It never applies or messages anyone for you.

  • Yours to take

    Your files are plain text, PDF and Word. With sync on, download it all as a ZIP.

Questions

Good to know.

What do I need?

A Mac or a Windows PC, and Claude Code signed in with your Claude plan or an API key. Factwright checks your setup when it opens and walks you through anything that’s missing.

What does it cost?

Factwright is free. The work Claude does counts against your own Claude plan or API key. To sync your other computers and your phone, our sync is $5 a month or $50 a year, or you can run your own sync server for free. If it helps your job hunt, you can buy its maker a coffee, but you never have to.

Does Factwright see my Claude login?

No. It starts the Claude Code you installed, as it is, and never reads, stores or passes your login. If you’re signed out, it tells you how to sign in yourself.

Who can see my job hunt?

Only you and your own Claude. Your fact bank, resumes and documents are plain files on your computer, and Claude Code reads them under your own Anthropic account when it works on them. If you turn on sync, they’re encrypted on your computer before they leave it, so our servers keep your job hunt only in a form they can’t read. On your phone, it opens in your browser with code our server sends, as our privacy policy explains.

Is Factwright open source?

Yes. All of its code is open, under the AGPL-3.0: the app, the skills your Claude gets, the web view and the sync server. It’s on GitHub, along with how its encryption works.

Can I run my own sync server?

Yes, for free, in a Cloudflare account of your own. It takes about 15 minutes, with no code to write, and Factwright and your phone sync through it, end-to-end encrypted, just as they do through ours. Our guide to running your own sync server walks you through it.

Will it make things up?

It’s built not to. Claims stay unconfirmed until you check them, resumes and answers use only confirmed facts, and every resume gets a second pass that checks each line against your fact bank. When a job asks for something you haven’t done, Claude asks you instead of guessing, and remembers your answer.

Does it apply to jobs for me?

No. Factwright gets everything ready: the resume, the cover letter, the answers, the messages. You read them, change what you like, and send them yourself.

Will it help me during an interview?

No. Before a call or an interview, it writes you a prep sheet: likely questions, answered from your facts, and the stories worth telling. In the interview itself, it’s all you.

Can I use my own resume template?

Yes. Show Factwright a resume whose look you like, as a PDF or a picture (PNG, JPEG or WebP), and your own Claude makes a template in that look: the layout, fonts, colors and spacing, never the words. You see it printed, with your name on it, before you pick it, and it stays private, on your computer. Save a Word file as a PDF first. A template another Factwright user shares with you can be imported too. A template changes how your resume looks, never what it says: apart from its section headings, every word comes from your facts.

Where does it look for jobs?

Hacker News’s monthly “Who is hiring?” thread, LinkedIn’s and Indeed’s listings, company job boards on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Gem, careers pages, and public listings such as Y Combinator’s Work at a Startup and Wellfound. It finds LinkedIn’s and Indeed’s listings through web search, then reads each job on the employer’s own site. You can also paste any posting, or add a job by hand. Our browser extension, Save to Factwright, coming soon, saves the one you’re looking at in Chrome or Firefox.

Can it use my LinkedIn?

Yes, without signing in. Save your profile with LinkedIn’s own “Save to PDF”, or download your data from LinkedIn. Factwright reads your profile and connections from it, so Claude can spot warm paths into a company.

Is Factwright made by Anthropic?

No. Factwright is an independent app by Neatifai LLC, Steve Hillyer’s company, and isn’t affiliated with Anthropic. It works with your own Claude account.
