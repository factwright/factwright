# Changelog

Notable changes to Factwright, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## Unreleased

### Added
- Your Claude works on several tasks at once, each in a run of its own, in a tab of Claude's panel, such as "Talkiatry · Tailored resume". Each tab has a dot that says where it stands, working, waiting on you, done or didn't finish, and its own bar, terminal and list of what it changed, with Undo. The bar sums them up beside the tabs, "3 running · 1 waiting on you · 1 in line", even with the panel folded. One task alone looks as before.
- Up to three tasks run at once, and the rest wait in line and start by themselves, in the order you asked. Your Claude → Settings → Tasks at once picks from one to five. Two tasks on the same job never run together, so a cover letter waits for that job's resume, and neither do two on your facts and Profile, or two job searches. A task's button says In line, the jobs list marks each job Claude is on or has in line, and Close on a task in line takes it out.
- Claude's questions and permission prompts name the run asking at the top, and with several waiting on you, the dialog says "1 of 3", goes through them in the order they came, and has Previous and Next to switch between them.
- On the job application sites you allow, the browser extension notices you submit an application. Once the page says it went in, or a form on one page moves on with no errors showing, the job is marked Applied, with what you sent for the form's own questions, page by page, and a note on the page offers Undo. Elsewhere, the popup has Mark as applied. Factwright takes it back for Undo while it's the job's latest change, through sync too, and keeps what you sent.
- A question's menu on the job application sites has Draft with Claude, where nothing on file answers it. It drafts all of the form's open questions at once, through the same draft path as the panel, says Drafting…, and each question's menu then offers its draft, to pick. Through sync, the questions are kept with the job, for your Claude in Factwright on your computer. For a job you haven't saved, it says to save it first.

### Changed
- Each task starts a run of its own, rather than going into the Claude Code that's open. You can still type a follow-up into a finished run's terminal until you close it. Another job's task no longer greys out the buttons: it waits in line, or starts beside it. Updates and switching profiles wait while any run has work going or a task waits in line.
- Claude Code's first-run screens and its question about trust come once: until one run is past them, only one starts at a time, and the rest start without them.
- Fit checks, salary checks and unmasked listings add their row to the job hunt's logs through Factwright's own tool, which writes one row at a time, so two runs never write a log at once. Your Claude can't edit those logs itself.
- Undo on a file another task changed too, side by side, such as a log or a fact file, asks first, as putting it back would undo that task's change as well.
- A run's name leads with the job's company, such as "Acme Robotics · Fit check", in its tab, its bar and the dialogs it asks in.
- Fill fills the form in its page, from the popup, the extension's Fill button, the right-click menu and the shortcut, and a small note on the page says how it went: "Factwright filled 14 fields · 2 need you · Details". The side panel opens only from Details or the popup, and shows that fill as it was, without filling it again over what you typed since. Save and fill is one click in Firefox too. On LinkedIn and Indeed, Fill opens the panel, with each answer to copy.
- The side panel is cleaner: the job at the top, then the fields that need you, then those it filled, folded away, each with where its answer came from in small type. Go to and Copy show on the row you point at, and Fill again and Mark as applied sit at the bottom.
- Fill puts its answer in place of what a field holds, wherever Factwright has one, on every form, Workday's, iCIMS's, Taleo's, SuccessFactors' and SmartRecruiters' newer form included. A set of boxes ends up with just Factwright's picks. A field it has no answer for, a box you agree with, a resume or cover letter you attached, and a part of the form the site filled with another job from your resume, stay as they are.
- A job's resume and cover letter go up under their own names, as your rule names them, not as "Your Name - Resume.pdf".
- A field's menu goes just under its field, over the page, where there's no room beside it, as a password manager's does, and a click in the field the cursor's in opens it.

### Fixed
- On Ashby, a Yes or No that Fill picked could look picked once Ashby's Autofill from resume had read a resume, while Ashby had taken it back, so Submit said it wasn't answered. Fill now puts files in first, waits while a page reads a resume, and checks each choice again a moment later, picking again any the page took back.
- On iCIMS's posting pages, and Lever's and Ashby's in a company's page, with the job application sites off, the popup offered Save and fill where there's no form yet. It offers Save.

## 0.17.0 - 2026-10-25

### Added
- Claude's questions have Skip. Send waits until every question has an answer or a skip, rather than sending once any one has an answer and leaving out the rest. Claude hears a skipped question as "The user skipped this question.", with your note if you add one, and a skip is never on record as your answer, so it backs no fact. A skipped question folds to say so, with Answer it to bring it back as you left it.
- The jobs list has an Added column: the day each job was found or saved, which sorts the list newest first, then oldest first.
- Each resume, cover letter and Projects page file has Download, which saves a copy where you pick, under its own name, and Show in Finder, or Show in folder on Windows, in its row and in its preview. The app does either only for a file the job lists.
- Once your Claude finishes a task, Claude's panel lists what it changed in your job hunt: each file it made or changed, by its own edits or through Factwright's tools, with how many lines, View to see it before and after, and Undo. Undo puts a file back as it was before the task, or moves a new one to the Trash, and asks first when the file changed again since. The list shows the pages your Claude read too. Factwright keeps the copies outside your job hunt, where your Claude can't reach them, for the last 20 tasks.
- A new number or a correction for a verified fact, which you give your Claude in answer to a question that names the fact, goes in the fact's place and keeps it verified, with your name and the date. What it gains, its words, numbers and tags, comes only from what you picked or typed, and it keeps the fact's "not" and "never".
- Quick resume, beside Tailor a resume, and Quick version, beside New version: a resume for the job that asks you nothing. Should your Claude ask anyway, Factwright turns the question away, and your Claude goes on without it. It uses verified facts only, leaving out the ones you haven't verified and the ones you're less sure of, and ends with a short list of what could make it stronger: the facts it left out, the gaps that matter and the numbers missing, in its notes and its last message. Quick version keeps the current resume first, as New version does. It runs at the tailored resume's model and effort.
- Profile's For applications has Why you're leaving: why you're leaving your job, or why you left your last one, in your own words. Filling a form puts it in "Reason for leaving", "Why are you looking for a new role?" and the other ways forms ask, and where a form asks it of each job, in the newest job's alone. What you sent before and your Claude's draft for the job wait beside it in the field's menu. Your Claude may fit it to a job when it drafts answers, but adds nothing it doesn't say. Filling a form no longer puts your job title or company in "Reason for leaving your current position", "…current employer" or any other question about why you're leaving, and a box for paragraphs never gets either. Mark as applied keeps what you sent for it with the job, so you can say the same in its interviews. With Factwright on the same computer, this release fills it. Filling through sync, and Mark as applied offering to keep a new answer to it in Profile, come with the extension's next version.
- Each job has a pay to ask for, in its Fit area: yours, which you can change there, or else your Claude's suggestion in the job's notes, or else your target pay for its kind of job. Filling a form puts it in pay questions: as you wrote it in a box to type in, one yearly amount in a box for a number, and in a choice of ranges, the one it falls in, which forms used to leave for you. A question about what you're paid now never gets it. With more than one kind of job, a job without its own gets its kind's target, not your first kind's. It syncs with the job, and the phone's page for the job shows it. Your Claude doesn't suggest one on its own yet. With Factwright on the same computer, this release fills it, and through sync, the extension's next version.
- The browser extension's popup saves a job in one click. It shows what it read, the job's title, company, pay, place and how long its posting is, with Save to Factwright, and Edit details shows each to check and change first, as the popup did before. A page that doesn't look like a job posting still shows its boxes, blank, with Save it anyway.
- The popup knows the jobs you saved. It looks the page up first, by its posting, its link, or its company and title, in Factwright on the same computer, or in what sync holds, whichever it saves to. A job you saved shows its title, company and status, with Fill this application for the page's form, and nothing to save but the page's posting, which a job saved without one takes. On the application of a job you haven't saved, one button, Save and fill, saves it and fills the form: by its link alone where the application page shows no posting of its own. In Chrome the panel opens and fills the form straight away; in Firefox, which opens its sidebar only from a click of its own, the popup offers Fill this application once the job's saved. Factwright on the same computer from before this release doesn't know how to look a job up, so there the popup offers to save every job, as it did. The privacy policy and the store text say what the look-up tells Factwright. This reaches the stores with the extension's next version, and this release carries the app's side: the look-up.
- The browser extension reads the pay a job's own job data doesn't give: Lever's from its salary section, and anywhere else, a clear range in the posting's words, with its currency and its year or hour, as "$161,000 - $245,000 a year", or iCIMS's "$144000.00 To $237500.00 / year". It reads an iCIMS posting in the frame its career site shows it in, from the frame's own job data, where the page around the frame is only the company's branding, whose title can be its blog's, and keeps the tab's own address as the job's link. Text you selected in a frame on the page counts as the posting too. This reaches the stores with the extension's next version.

### Changed
- A run that's done, or ready with nothing to do, shows Close in place of Stop. Close quits Claude Code and puts the bar away in one click. Stop stays for a run that's starting, working or waiting on you. A turn you interrupt in the terminal reads done once Claude Code says it's waiting for you, about a minute later, rather than working for good.
- Updates and switching profiles wait only while Claude is working or waits on you, not whenever Claude Code is open. Restart now quits a Claude Code that's done first, then installs, and says Restarting… meanwhile.
- The jobs list never hides a column's heading, so you can sort by Next step and Pay at any width. When the window is too narrow for every column, the list scrolls sideways, with the jobs' names pinned and a fade at the edge where there's more. Until you pick a job, no panel takes room beside the list.
- Your Claude changes your job hunt's files and reads web pages without asking, never LinkedIn's. Your facts change only through Factwright's fact tools, which take only what you answered: outside getting started and adding facts, your Claude can't edit them any other way. Nor can it edit your notes, the files you shared, your LinkedIn data, Profile's files, which change only through Factwright's tools, or the instructions Claude Code loads. Saving to your Profile and saving a template don't ask either, apart from a template a revision would change. So Your Claude → Settings no longer has Change any file in your job hunt or Read any website.
- Saving a job from the browser, on the same computer or through sync, finds it in your job hunt as Factwright's own matching does, by its posting, its link, or its company and title, rather than by its link and the ID its company and title make alone. The same posting is already in your job hunt, so a save from Ashby's or Lever's application page finds the job saved from its posting. The same role at the same company, where the one job on file with it has no posting of its own, only a job board's address, a Hacker News post or the company's site, gets this page's link and posting, so a job a Hacker News search found by its company's whole Workable board no longer gets a "-2" copy when you save it from its posting. Another posting of the same role is still a job of its own. Workable's postings are now known by their own ID, as `apply.workable.com/<company>/j/<id>`, the same on their application.
- The browser extension's popup says when a page doesn't look like a job posting, such as a search engine's results, a weather page or a news article. It fills in no job title or company from such a page, so it isn't saved as a job by mistake, and Save it anyway saves it with the title and company you type. It goes by a job's signs: the job data job sites publish, a job site or applicant system it knows, an application form, words about jobs in the page's address or title, and the things postings say, in English and as German, French, Spanish and Dutch ones most often put them, such as their duties, the qualifications they ask for, their pay and their equal-opportunity line. A page needs two of these, or one where it shows no text to go by, and a search engine's results never look like a job's, whatever they say. The right-click menu and the shortcut leave such a page for the popup, now text selected on a search engine's results too, which they used to save at once, and in Firefox the button's "!" says why. Once you've cleared a job's company and tried to save, the popup no longer puts back the one its address names. This reaches the stores with the extension's next version.

## 0.14.0 - 2026-10-23

### Added
- Filling an application uses your resumes for what Profile leaves blank: your name, email, phone, where you live, LinkedIn and website, and where you work now and as what. They come from the job's own tailored resume, or your newest for a job that has none yet. Where a form asks for them by name, it also fills your school, degree, field of study and graduation year, and your skills, certifications and summary. The extension's panel says which resume each answer came from, and a resume field on a job with no tailored resume says so, rather than "Not in Profile yet". This reaches the stores with the extension's next version.
- Profile's About you has Fill in from your resume, once Factwright has made a resume: one click fills in what's blank there from your newest resume, and leaves what you've typed as it is.
- Profile has Work history and education: each company you worked at, with every position you held there, newest first, so three positions at one company stay three, and each school. Fill in with Claude fills them in from your fact bank and resumes, and you can change them. Filling an application uses them where a form asks for each job and school one at a time: each block takes the next position, with its dates as the form asks for them, by month and year, typed, or as Present.
- Your Claude drafts what nothing on file answers. When a form asks questions Factwright has no answer for, the extension's panel offers Draft them with your Claude. Your Claude drafts them in Factwright's window, without it coming to the front: a choice as one of its options, word for word, and an answer under the form's limit. The panel fills each draft in once it's done, and leaves what only you can answer for you, with your Claude's reason. The questions are kept with the job, so its panel in Factwright shows them with the drafts.
- The extension reads which part of a form each field is in, such as Work experience or Education 2. So a block's Start date is that job's or that school's, a date asked as Month and Year boxes is filled in both, and a reference's or emergency contact's Name and Phone are never filled with yours.

### Changed
- The extension's panel says why it left a field for you: a date to pick on a calendar, a box to tick, or a job you still hold, which has no end date. These reach the stores with the extension's next version, as the fill changes above do.

### Fixed
- Sync no longer says Syncing… for minutes when a request to the sync server stalls. Each request gives up after a minute, longer for a big file, and sync says it's offline and tries again.
- When you've held more than one title at one company, such as after a promotion, your resumes show each role as it was: a job of its own, with the same company and its own title, dates and bullets, never merged into one, and never with an older title made a heading over bullets. Getting started asks about every title you held at each employer, and your fact bank keeps one file for each employer, with a section for each role, newest first, and that role's facts under it. The exact titles your resumes must show list every title you held at each company, and building a resume checks each of its jobs there against them. Before, it checked only the first job at a company, against one title, so a right title could be flagged as wrong and your Claude could change it. A rule from before, with one title, still reads as it did.
- A fact your Claude saves from your answer while it works on a job goes under the role it's about, in the file of an employer where you held more than one title. Before, it went after the file's last fact, under your earliest role there. A section for a role the file doesn't have yet is started only in words the file or your answer has, so a run can't give you a title you never held.
- The ATS X-ray finds the company of each job on a resume that names the company once, above the titles held there, with or without the company's own dates. Before, it said No company found for each of those jobs, or listed the company as a job of its own. A bullet with no full stop is never read as a job's company either.

- Re-running a release's failed jobs long after it began can no longer put an older version out over a newer one that's gone out since.
- A release whose uploads stopped partway can be finished by re-running its failed jobs, instead of needing a new version. Each file goes up with its sha256, so a re-run leaves the files that are up, and refuses one with other contents. A version that's been taken back, as docs/releasing.md says, can never be put out again, or get a release on GitHub, by re-running its release.

### Changed
- The sync server deletes sign-in records it no longer needs, each day: sessions that ended, sign-in steps that ran out, and the sign-in limits' counts, which are kept by IP address.
- The sync server keeps less: a session keeps its device's name in place of everything the browser or app sent, and no IP address. GitHub's and Google's tokens and the profile picture link aren't kept, and Cloudflare's log of every request is off for the sync server and the website. A ticket to listen for changes no longer shows which account it's for.
- Deleting an account, or approving a device's code, needs a session from a browser's sign-in with GitHub or Google, sign-in's routes that list sessions or sign others out are off, and the account key's locked copy can't be replaced once saved. So someone who tricks you into approving their device code can't delete your account, approve codes of their own, take another session's sign-in, or spoil your recovery key. Sessions from before this sign in again in the browser before deleting or approving.
- Deleting an account signs out every device first, and the account's store then turns everything away, so nothing can fill it again. An upload the store turns away is deleted at once, and a deletion that failed partway finishes when tried again.
- app.factwright.com tells browsers to keep to https.
- The web view names each job's page by a code made with the account's key, so its address no longer shows the company and role to the server or the browser's history. It forgets its keys when it finds it was signed out from another device, by running out, or by the account being deleted, and a page left open forgets them and what it shows the next time it hears from the server. An older address that named a company is replaced once the job hunt opens, so it isn't left in the address bar or the tab's back list. When Factwright can't be reached, the web view says so, with a way to try again.

### Fixed
- Signing in to sync again checks the account's key before anything syncs, and asks again after a restart until it has. An account deleted and made again gets a new account key and recovery key, which the old ones and any lost device can't open. Another account that uses another key is linked to only when asked, with **Use a different account** to end that sign-in instead.
- A computer that's syncing checks that the account still uses its key. 0.1.0 could sync a deleted-and-remade account under its old key while a newer computer used a new one: each skipped what the other sent, while both said they were up to date. Now that computer stops and does what signing in again does: it makes a new account key and recovery key when the account has none, or links again to the account's new key.
- Each sync step that waits on the server gives up once another sign-in, or closing the app, overtakes it, so a late answer can't change the sign-in that came after it.
