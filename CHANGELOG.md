# Changelog

Notable changes to Factwright, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## Unreleased

### Added
- A job's status says what you decided, in the same words as the views. New is a job nobody has touched yet: opening it doesn't count. Claude's work on it, such as a fit check, a salary check, a question or a resume, moves it to To review, and only you move it further: To apply, Later or Passed, then Applied onward. Later is for a job you've looked at and set aside: it folds at the bottom of To review, outside its count, and a day you give it brings it back up, marked "Back from Later". Role closed is a new way for a job to end, for a posting that came down or a role that was filled, cancelled or frozen, before or after you applied.
- A new job's panel leads with Run a fit check and four quick choices: To review, To apply, Later and Pass. Passing asks why, in one click (pay, level, location, not your kind of work, the company, or something else), and job searches read it: they flag a passed job they find again, and suggest a deal-breaker when the same reason keeps coming up.
- Select several jobs in the list, or a run of them with Shift, to move or pass them all at once. A bar under the list moves them, then asks why you passed or when the jobs set aside come back, with Undo.
- Accepting an offer offers to withdraw from your other jobs still in play, in one step. It marks them Withdrawn in Factwright only, so you still tell each company yourself.
- A Support Factwright link at the bottom of the sidebar, above the version, which opens the Buy Me a Coffee page of Factwright's maker, Steve Hillyer, in your browser, for anyone who'd like to chip in.
- factwright.com says Factwright is fully open source: a GitHub link in its header and under the download buttons, a section on what anyone can check in the code, with the folders worth opening first, a question about it, and a link to the code in every page's footer. It also says who builds Factwright, and how to support it with a coffee or on GitHub Sponsors.
- Each release appears on the repository's Releases page on GitHub, with its notes. While the repository is public, its installers also come with a signed record of the commit and run that built them, which `gh attestation verify` checks.

### Changed
- The jobs page has seven views: New, To review, To apply, Applied, Interviewing, Closed and All. A fit check or a tailored resume no longer moves a job to To apply. Claude's work shows in the job's fit score and next step instead, such as "Fit check says skip. Pass?" or "Resume ready. Apply or pass?". The status menu groups the statuses into before applying, after applying, and closed.
- Checked and Resume ready are no longer statuses: a job that had either reads as To review, and nothing tracked about it changes. Computers still on an earlier version show a job with one of the new statuses by Claude's work, and keep its status as it is, until they update: restart Factwright on each to update it.
- Only Factwright's own releases sync through app.factwright.com and look for updates at downloads.factwright.com. A copy built anywhere else, such as from a fork, has neither: its Sync card says sync isn't in that copy, getting started doesn't offer sync, and it never looks for updates, unless its builder sets up servers of their own.
- factwright.com, the README and the terms say Factwright is free, with no "during the beta": it stays free, with support from those who'd like to give. The terms no longer say what would happen if it started charging.
- The terms say Factwright's code is under the AGPL-3.0, which they don't limit, and that their rules cover the services Neatifai runs. Supporting Factwright goes to Steve Hillyer, isn't a payment to Neatifai, and buys nothing. The privacy policy says what supporting it shares, and with whom.

### Fixed
- Re-running a release's failed jobs long after it began can no longer put an older version out over a newer one that's gone out since.
- A release whose uploads stopped partway can be finished by re-running its failed jobs, instead of needing a new version. Each file goes up with its sha256, so a re-run leaves the files that are up, and refuses one with other contents. A version that's been taken back, as docs/releasing.md says, can never be put out again, or get a release on GitHub, by re-running its release.

## 0.1.1 - 2026-10-13

Steadier sync, the version in the sidebar, the privacy policy and terms, and installers in Factwright's colors.

### Added
- A privacy policy and terms of service, at factwright.com/privacy and factwright.com/terms. Every page of the site links to them. Downloading, the app's first step and signing in to sync each say they mean agreeing to the terms, and the privacy policy says what's collected.
- The operator can delete an account for someone who asked by email, with a token only the operator has.
- The sidebar shows Factwright's version at the bottom, so you can see an update has landed.
- The Mac disk image opens on a window in Factwright's colors, with an arrow showing where to drag the app: onto Applications.
- Installing on Windows shows a window in Factwright's colors: a blue header with the mark, "Installing Factwright…", and a thin progress bar. It's sharp at every display scaling, and updates still install silently.

### Changed
- The sync server deletes sign-in records it no longer needs, each day: sessions that ended, sign-in steps that ran out, and the sign-in limits' counts, which are kept by IP address.
- The sync server keeps less: a session keeps its device's name in place of everything the browser or app sent, and no IP address. GitHub's and Google's tokens and the profile picture link aren't kept, and Cloudflare's log of every request is off for the sync server and the website. A ticket to listen for changes no longer shows which account it's for.
- Deleting an account, or approving a device's code, needs a session from a browser's sign-in with GitHub or Google, sign-in's routes that list sessions or sign others out are off, and the account key's locked copy can't be replaced once saved. So someone who tricks you into approving their device code can't delete your account, approve codes of their own, take another session's sign-in, or spoil your recovery key. Sessions from before this sign in again in the browser before deleting or approving.
- Deleting an account signs out every device first, and the account's store then turns everything away, so nothing can fill it again. An upload the store turns away is deleted at once, and a deletion that failed partway finishes when tried again.
- app.factwright.com tells browsers to keep to https.
- The web view names each job's page by a code made with the account's key, so its address no longer shows the company and role to the server or the browser's history. It forgets its keys when it finds it was signed out from another device, by running out, or by the account being deleted, and a page left open forgets them and what it shows the next time it hears from the server. An older address that named a company is replaced once the job hunt opens, so it isn't left in the address bar or the tab's back list. When Factwright can't be reached, the web view says so, with a way to try again.

### Fixed
- Signing in to sync again checks the account's key before anything syncs, and asks again after a restart until it has. An account deleted and made again gets a new account key and recovery key, which the old ones and any lost device can't open. Another account that uses another key is linked to only when asked, with **Use a different account** to end that sign-in instead.
- A computer that's syncing checks that the account still uses its key. 0.1.0 could sync a deleted-and-remade account under its old key while a newer computer used a new one: each skipped what the other sent, while both said they were up to date. Now that computer stops and does what signing in again does: it makes a new account key and recovery key when the account has none, or links again to the account's new key.
- Each sync step that waits on the server gives up once another sign-in, or closing the app, overtakes it, so a late answer can't change the sign-in that came after it.

- `@factwright/core`: fit tiers, from strong to skip.
- The desktop app's window, built from the design system. It runs sandboxed and loads only the app's own files over an `app://` address. The sidebar shows whether your Claude Code is installed, up to date and signed in, and what to do when it isn't. `pnpm dev` runs it.
- `@factwright/ui`: the logo and an empty-state block.
- Claude's panel: your own Claude Code runs live in the app, locked down. It runs with no shell, only Factwright's plugin and settings, and no claude.ai connectors. Its status reads Starting, Working, Needs your answer, Done or Stopped, from Claude Code's hooks. The panel tucks away and comes back when Claude needs you, with a notification if you're in another app.
- `@factwright/ui`: the terminal's colors, checked against WCAG AA in the roles Claude Code gives them, and a Starting state for Claude's status.
- The plugin folder has its manifest, so Claude Code loads it.
- `@factwright/core`: reading the workspace into jobs, ported from the original dashboard. It reads the fit check log, each job's folder, job searches, salary and employer checks, and drafted messages, and joins them into one job each. It also reads a fit check's score breakdown and what people say about the company.
- Tracking in the job panel: a next step with a date, logging calls and interviews (which move a job forward), notes that save as you type, history with dates you can fix and an Undo, and archiving.
- Adding a job by hand, filters (fit, source, minimum pay, has a resume, archived), and Your journey: how far your jobs got and how they ended, with a flow chart for sankeymatic.com and a spreadsheet to save.
- `@factwright/ui`: form fields, menus, checkboxes, filter chips and dialogs.
- `@factwright/core`: each job's journey, its flow chart and its spreadsheet. Spreadsheet cells that start like a formula are kept as text.
- The jobs screen, as the approved mockup shows it: the numbers that matter, views, search, and each job's next step, with a panel for everything on file for a job. You can change a job's status there, read its documents, and open its resumes.
- `@factwright/core`: where each job stands, what needs your attention, the stats and views, and the apply steps from `apply.md`.
- `@factwright/ui`: the status menu, score bars, the "what people say" badge, a red tint, and a safe Markdown view for documents.
- Email links, such as how to apply by email, open your mail app.
- The desktop app reads your jobs from the workspace, and reads them again when something changes. Your tracker keeps statuses with their dates, applied dates, next steps, notes and calls, in a small database beside the workspace. Logging a call moves a job forward, and you can undo a mis-click.
- Factwright's tools for Claude, over MCP: `find_job` (whether you've seen a job before), `job_details` (everything on file, including your notes and calls) and `save_posting` (a posting saved word for word from Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Gem or the page). Each run gets them from a server on this computer that answers only that run. The two that read run without asking; Claude Code asks you before each posting it fetches, and fetching reaches only public https sites.
- `@factwright/core`: reading a job posting as plain text, from an applicant system's API or a web page.
- The first seven skills, ported from the original repo: fit check, add facts, answer application questions, interview prep, salary check, unmask a job, and who to message. They use Factwright's tools instead of scripts and git, and save their work where the app reads it.
- `read_page`: Claude reads a public web page through Factwright, which asks you first. `find_job` works from a posting's link alone. Web searches and Factwright's skills run without asking.
- Run with your Claude: each job's panel starts a tailored resume, a fit check, a salary check, who to message, interview prep or the application's questions on that job, in Claude's panel, and shows where each stands. The app types the command itself, naming the job only by its ID.
- Tailored resumes: the `/factwright:focused-resume` skill, ported from the original repo, and a `build_resume` tool that builds a job's resume as a PDF or Word file. It checks the resume against your rules and confidential terms, says how it fits the pages, and keeps the files that went out with an application.
- `@factwright/engine`: the resume engine, ported from the original repo. It lays out a resume as a PDF-ready page in three layouts and as a Word file, checks it against your rules and confidential terms, and measures how its lines fit. A resume can name the heading over its systems cards.
- Job searches: the `/factwright:job-search` skill, ported from the original repo, and a Find jobs button that starts it. Two tools feed it: `hn_jobs` saves every post in Hacker News's "Who is hiring?" thread, and `job_board` saves every open job on a company's Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or Gem board and says whether a posting is still open. Both save word for word in the search's folder, and run without asking, since they send only a board's name or a month to fixed public APIs. The jobs that fit show up under To review.
- `@factwright/core`: reading whole job boards and Hacker News's hiring thread.
- Resumes look the same on a Mac as on Windows: each one carries open fonts drawn to the widths of the Windows fonts the layouts use (Carlito, renamed Factwright Sans, for Calibri; Caladea for Cambria; Selawik for Segoe UI; and Barlow in place of Bahnschrift). Printed on a Mac, a resume made on Windows breaks every line in the same place.
- Application questions: the job panel's Application questions asks for the form's questions first, one box each, the way the original dashboard did. They're kept with the job in your tracker, and Claude reads them through `job_details`, never as typed commands. The button reads Ran once answers are on file.
- The setup check: once for each version of Claude Code, which updates itself, the app starts it hidden with no prompt, so it uses none of your plan. It checks that Claude Code reaches Factwright's tools and that its hooks arrive. A failure shows in the sidebar's Your Claude card, with Check again.
- The first run's trust question, explained: when Claude Code asks whether to trust the workspace, the run reads "Needs your answer", and a note says how to answer. It also explains that the warning about running files doesn't apply, since Claude Code has no shell here.
- Resume previews: each resume PDF in a job's panel has Preview, which shows its pages in the app, drawn by pdf.js, with a way to open it in your PDF app.
- End-to-end tests: Playwright drives the built app through its main flows on a made-up job hunt. A stand-in for Claude Code answers the app's checks and runs, through the same hooks and tools, and never reaches Anthropic. They run in CI on every change.
- Windows checks: a weekly workflow runs the unit and end-to-end tests on Windows, ahead of the first run on a Windows PC.
- `@factwright/sync`: end-to-end encryption and the sync protocol. An account key made on your first device, with keys for each purpose made from it; a recovery key; link codes for adding a device; locking with AES-256-GCM, bound to what each value is; and merging two devices' changes to the same row. It uses only Web Crypto. The key design is written up in the security doc.
- The sync server (`apps/server`), a Cloudflare Worker. Sign-in with GitHub or Google through Better Auth, and a device code you approve in your browser for the desktop app. Each account gets its own store, a Durable Object with its own database, plus a folder of encrypted files in R2. It keeps each record's history for 90 days, stores each file's contents once, holds link codes for adding a device, tells listening devices about changes as they happen, backs every store up daily, and cleans up after itself. It sees only locked data, and tests try to reach across accounts through every route.
- Sync in the desktop app. The sidebar's Sync card turns it on: you sign in through your browser, the first computer shows your recovery key to save, and every other computer links with a code from one that's set up, or a QR code for a phone. Files and the tracker sync a moment after each change, and right away when another device changes something. When two devices change the same thing, nothing is lost: a file keeps both copies, and a tracked job merges. The session and the account key are kept in the system's keychain. The Sync dialog lists your devices and signs any of them out.
- The job panel's notes show changes made elsewhere, unless you're typing in them.
- The web view (`apps/web`), for your phone or any browser. Sign in, link it by scanning the QR code a computer shows (or typing its code, or with your recovery key), and your job hunt opens right in the browser: the same numbers, views, search and next steps as the desktop app, and each job's notes, calls, history, documents and resumes. The account page lists your devices, downloads a copy of everything as a ZIP, and deletes the account. The sync server serves it, and it's where you let the desktop app sign in. Added to a phone's home screen, it has its own icon.
- `@factwright/core`: building the job list from the tracker's rows, and the date, size and pay formats, shared by the desktop app and the web view.
- The sync server turns away changes a signed-in browser sends from another site.

### Changed
- Factwright's new mark: two lines of a resume on one thread, traced down to a dot, the fact behind them. It's in the top bar, getting started and the web view, a heavier cut keeps it crisp at small sizes, and the browser tab and home-screen icons are drawn from it.
- The app has its own icon, the mark on a blue rounded square, and shows it in the Dock while you develop. Installers will build it in.
- `@factwright/ui/brand`: the mark's shapes, and the app icon drawn as a PNG with no browser or image library.
- Resume templates of your own. The app comes with two plain ones, Classic and Minimal, in place of eight. Bring a resume whose look you like, as a PDF or a picture, and your Claude makes a template in that look: its layout, fonts and colors, never its words. It checks a printed preview against the original as it goes (`/factwright:make-template`). A template file someone shared works too. Your templates live in your workspace's `templates/` folder, sync like everything else, and sit beside the app's in the picker, where you can remove them. Claude reads and saves them with `list_templates`, `read_template` and `save_template`.
- `@factwright/engine`: a template is data rather than code: its styles, markup in Mustache's syntax that the engine fills in, its fonts, section names and look in Word, in one JSON file. The engine checks each one before using it, so a template from anywhere can only lay out text, and adds no words of its own: every word comes from the resume or the template's section titles. Printed pages run no scripts. Classic and Minimal print exactly as before.
- A run started to make a template reads and saves templates without asking, and nothing else: it can't reach the web, and asks before building a resume, saving an answer or changing any file, since the resume it copies came from anywhere. It can replace only the templates it saved itself. Replacing one of yours in any other run puts the one before in the trash.
- The jobs list in the stages a job moves through: To review, To apply, Applied, Interviewing (screens, interviews and offers, each job with its calls so far) and Closed, then All, in place of Active. It opens on the view you last picked.
- Find jobs asks where to look: Hacker News's "Who is hiring?" thread (the newest or a month), LinkedIn, Indeed, companies' job boards, or a company you name. LinkedIn's and Indeed's listings are found through web search and read on each employer's own site: Factwright's tools never read LinkedIn's pages. Each place is a search of its own, and **Found by** narrows the list to one search's jobs, such as the month's HN thread. Jobs to review say which search found them.
- The job panel: sections that open and close and stay as you left them, a header that stays in view, and **Expand**, which gives a job the whole window in two columns, with the previous and next jobs a click away.
- Sync catches up once when an update adds a table that syncs, such as notes, so what other devices sent while this one couldn't keep it still arrives. It also never deletes rows of a table only a newer version keeps.
- Progress: each step a job has taken, from found to where it is now, with its date, and the step ahead. The dates of the steps you marked, and the applied date, can be fixed in place: it's where a status marked a few days late gets put right.
- Dated notes: jot down what was said on a call or in an interview as you go. Each note is dated today, or the day you pick, newest first, and syncs. Your notes from before stay at the end. Claude reads them all with `job_details`, so interview prep and tailored resumes can use them.
- Your journey draws its flow chart in the app, as a view beside the summary. Copying it for sankeymatic.com stays.
- `@factwright/core`: the flow chart's layout, each job's search runs, dated notes, and the views' names and order.
- The fact bank has a page of its own. Read your facts file by file, search them all at once, or see together the ones still to confirm. A fact changes in a form: its title, the words resumes use, its status, ownership and confidence, and its details. You can also confirm a fact, delete one, add one, or change the sections around them, such as an overview. A change touches only that fact, and the rest of its file stays exactly as it was. If Claude or another computer changes a fact while you have it open, yours waits until you save again. Your Claude goes by your changes from its next task.
- `@factwright/core`: reading the fact bank's files a heading at a time, and changing, adding, deleting or confirming a claim while the rest of its file stays as written.
- When more than one of your templates fits a job, such as two for any job, a tailored resume uses the one that suits the role, by what your guide says each is for, and says which it picked.
- Installers: one Mac disk image for Apple silicon and Intel, and a Windows installer that needs no administrator. Releases are signed and notarized, then published to downloads.factwright.com by a workflow when a version is tagged. The installed app is locked down with Electron's fuses, and keeps the same data folder as before. Factwright's first version is 0.1.0.
- Updates: an installed copy looks for a new version every ten minutes, and as soon as you come back to it, downloads it in the background, and shows **Update ready** at the bottom of the sidebar, with what's new. It installs when you restart, or the next time you quit, and asks first if you're writing a fact or a note you haven't saved.
- The website offers the download for your computer, with the other one a link under it.
