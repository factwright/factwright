# Style: public-sector

## Reader
Government agencies, nonprofits, schools and universities, and public health systems. A screener, often in HR, checks the resume against each qualification the posting lists, sometimes with a scoring sheet. Then a hiring panel reads it. They reward:
- each listed qualification, met in plain words they can find
- service and mission: who the work helped
- stewardship: budgets, grants, compliance and accountability, as far as `facts/` allows
- credentials: degrees, licenses and certifications, exactly as held

The look comes from `templates.md`, for government, nonprofits and schools.

## Emphasize
- **The posting's qualifications, one by one,** in its own words where they're true. Screeners look for them.
- **Who the work served:** students, patients, residents, members, donors.
- **Accountability:** compliance, audits, reporting, policy followed or written.
- **Credentials,** exactly as held, with license numbers left out.

## Avoid
- Sales language: "crushed", "hustled", "disrupted".
- Leaving out a qualification because it seems obvious. If it isn't written, it isn't counted.

## Government jobs
- For a US federal job (USAJOBS), follow the posting: give each job's start and end month and year, and its hours per week when the posting asks, and keep to two pages.
- State and local jobs often have their own application forms: answers go there, and the resume backs them up.

## Tone
Clear, plain and complete. Specific over impressive.

## Sections
Header → Summary → highlights → Experience → Education and credentials → Skills → service and volunteering.
