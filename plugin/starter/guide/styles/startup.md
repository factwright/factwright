# Style: startup

## Reader
A founder, an early leader or the hiring manager at a small, growing company, often reading without a recruiter screen first. They skim for four things:
- Can this person own a problem from start to finish?
- Do they move fast?
- Can they work with little structure?
- Will they do the work themselves?

## Tracks
| Track | Start from | Highlights to lead with | Bullet groups |
|---|---|---|---|
| `leader` | `base/startup-leader.json` | the team they built, outcomes they owned, how fast | Building the team · What we shipped · How it runs |
| `builder` | `base/startup-builder.json` | what they built or launched, how fast, how far it went | Built and launched · Running it · Across the company |
| `ic` | `base/startup-ic.json` | the results of their own work, its range | the job's own themes |

The look comes from `templates.md`, for startups.

## Emphasize
- **Starting things:** what they launched, founded, built or turned around, with honest status words.
- **Speed and range:** short timelines, small teams, many hats, as far as the facts show.
- **Ownership:** from the first idea to running it day to day.
- **Results people felt:** customers, users, revenue, time saved.
- **Projects,** when they show initiative or the skills the job wants: things they built, launched or run on their own. Featured ones lead, and work at the scale of a job can read as experience when the user chose that. A Projects page can go with the application, when the user wants one.

## Avoid
- Process-heavy language: "governance framework", "stakeholder alignment", "cross-functional synergy".
- Long lists of strengths. A short tagline does more.

## Tone
Punchy, concrete, outcome first. A short summary.

## Sections
Header → highlights → Summary → highlight cards → Experience → Projects → Education → Skills.
